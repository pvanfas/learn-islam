# How to Use This Guide

Part: Start Here

---

## Who This Guide Is For

This guide serves four audiences:

**Complete beginners** who know nothing about Islam and want an honest, comprehensive introduction from the very first question "what is Islam?" through the foundational beliefs, practices, history, and daily life of a Muslim.

**New Muslims** who have taken their Shahada and need structured, reliable knowledge to build their practice on firm foundations.

**Practicing Muslims** who grew up with the religion but want to understand why the theology, history, and evidence behind the practices they perform.

**Serious students** who want to move beyond basics into Islamic law, spirituality, history, and scholarship.

---

The guide is divided into 18 Parts, moving from foundational to advanced:

| Parts 3–5   | The tradition, the scripture, and its sciences     |

| Parts 8–10  | Islamic law, dress, and the purity of practice     |
| Parts 11–14 | Responding to criticism, how Islam spread, reverts |
| Parts 15–16 | Eschatology and spirituality                       |
| Part 17     | Practical daily Muslim life                        |
| Part 18     | Advanced scholarship                               |

---

## Three Learning Paths

**The New Muslim Path (3-6 months):**
Start with Part 1 (What is Islam), then Part 2 (Beliefs), then Part 4 (Five Pillars practical chapters), then Part 17 (Living as a Muslim). Return to the rest as you settle in.

**The Curious Non-Muslim Path:**
Start with Part 1, then Part 6 (Seerah the Prophet's life), then Part 11 (Responses to common questions), then wherever your curiosity leads.

**The Student of Knowledge Path:**
Work through all 18 parts in order. Take notes. Follow up the reading lists in the Appendix. Find a local teacher.

---

## Using the Difficulty Levels

Each chapter is marked with a level:

- Beginner: Accessible to anyone with no prior knowledge
- Intermediate: Assumes basic familiarity with Islamic vocabulary
- Advanced: For students with solid foundation in the deen

---

## A Note on Madhab Differences

Where scholars of the four Sunni schools differ on a ruling, this guide notes the difference clearly. It does not declare one madhab correct and dismiss others. Readers should follow the ruling of their own qualified scholar for personal practice.
