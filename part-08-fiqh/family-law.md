## In This Chapter

> Islamic family law (_Fiqh al-Usrah_) covers the full arc of family life: the marriage contract, the rights and responsibilities within marriage, the processes and types of divorce, custody of children, and inheritance. This chapter provides a clear overview of each area, noting where the four madhabs differ and where they agree.

## The Marriage Contract (_Nikah_)

For full detail on the marriage contract, its pillars, and the rights within marriage, see the [Marriage in Islam](../part-17-living-as-muslim/marriage.md) chapter in Part 17. Here we focus on the legal structure.

**Valid Nikah requires:**

1. **Offer and Acceptance** (_Ijab wa Qabul_) the Wali offers on behalf of the bride; the groom accepts
2. **Guardian (_Wali_)** obligatory in Shafi'i and Hanbali; the woman can contract herself in Hanafi and Maliki with conditions
3. **Two witnesses** two adult, sane, Muslim males
4. **Mahr** specified in the contract; belongs exclusively to the wife

**What makes a marriage invalid:**

- Absence of the Wali (Shafi'i/Hanbali)
- Lack of witnesses
- Marriage to a woman already married
- Marriage within the prohibited degrees of relation

## Types of Divorce

### 1 Talaq (Husband-Initiated)

The husband has the right to pronounce Talaq. Islamic law specifies how this must be done to be valid and revocable.

**The Sunnah method of Talaq:**

- Pronounced once, while the wife is in a state of purity (not menstruating)
- The couple then observes the 'Iddah (waiting period)
- During the 'Iddah, the husband may revoke the Talaq verbally or through resuming marital relations
- If he does not revoke it the divorce is final after the 'Iddah

**The three-Talaq rule:**

- After the first revocable Talaq and 'Iddah: the couple may reconcile or part
- After a second: same option
- After a third Talaq: the divorce is irrevocable they cannot remarry unless she genuinely marries another man, that marriage ends, and she observes a new 'Iddah

**Prohibited forms:**

- Pronouncing all three Talaqs at once considered sinful, though the madhabs differ on whether it counts as one or three
- Pronouncing Talaq while the wife is menstruating (_Talaq Bid'i_) forbidden but valid according to most scholars

### 2 Khul' (Wife-Initiated Divorce)

The wife has the right to initiate divorce by returning the Mahr to the husband. This requires:

- Her requesting it
- The husband accepting (OR a judge granting it if the husband refuses and harm is established)
- She returns the Mahr she received

**Why Khul' exists:** The right to Khul' ensures that a woman who is unable to live with her husband is not trapped. The Quran states clearly that keeping a woman against her will to harm her is prohibited.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Baqarah 2:229**

**Translation:** "...And it is not lawful for you to take back from them anything of what you have given them unless both fear that they will not keep within the limits of Allah. And if you fear that they will not keep within the limits of Allah, there is no blame upon either of them concerning that by which she ransoms herself..."
{% endhint %}

### 3 Faskh (Judicial Annulment)

A judge may annul a marriage when:

- The husband is unable or unwilling to provide financially
- The husband is absent for an extended period without news
- The husband has an incurable disease that prevents the marriage from functioning
- There is established harm to the wife

## The 'Iddah (Waiting Period)

After divorce or a husband's death, the wife observes a waiting period before she may remarry:

| Situation                                 | 'Iddah Period        |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Divorced woman (not pregnant)             | 3 menstrual cycles   |
| Divorced woman (pregnant)                 | Until delivery       |
| Widow (not pregnant)                      | 4 months and 10 days |
| Widow (pregnant)                          | Until delivery       |
| Woman who has not menstruated (young/old) | 3 months             |

**Wisdom of the 'Iddah:**

- Establishes paternity if pregnancy exists
- Allows time for reconciliation in revocable divorce
- Honors the solemnity of the marriage's ending
- Ensures the woman does not enter a new marriage while pregnant from the previous

## Custody (_Hadanah_)

When a marriage ends, custody of children follows Islamic rules:

**General principle:** The mother has priority for custody of young children for nurturing, care, and daily needs. The father retains _guardianship_ (_Wilayah_) the authority over major life decisions.

**Age thresholds (vary by madhab):**

- **Hanafi:** Mother retains custody until boys are 79 and girls reach puberty (approximately)
- **Maliki:** Mother retains custody until boys reach puberty and girls marry
- **Shafi'i:** After age 78, the child chooses between parents
- **Hanbali:** Boys at 7, girls at 9 then they choose

**Conditions for the mother to retain custody:**

- She has not remarried a non-Mahram to the child
- She is mentally and physically capable
- She lives within a reasonable distance

**The child's best interest is always primary:** If any condition harming the child is established, custody is reassigned to whoever can best provide for the child's welfare.

## Inheritance (_Mirath_)

Islamic inheritance law is one of the most detailed legal systems in the Quran unusual, given that most Quranic legislation is stated as principles rather than specifics.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah An-Nisa 4:1112**

These two verses contain the detailed Quranic formula for inheritance specifying exact fractions for different relatives.
{% endhint %}

**Fixed shares (_Fard_) for specified heirs:**

| Heir                                | Share |
| ----------------------------------- | ----- |
| Husband (if no children)            | 1/2   |
| Husband (with children)             | 1/4   |
| Wife (if no children)               | 1/4   |
| Wife (with children)                | 1/8   |
| Daughter (sole)                     | 1/2   |
| Two or more daughters               | 2/3   |
| Son's daughter (if no daughter)     | 1/2   |
| Mother (if no children or siblings) | 1/3   |
| Mother (with children or siblings)  | 1/6   |
| Father (if children exist)          | 1/6   |

The son receives double the daughter's share when they inherit together the classic area of critical scrutiny.

**The Islamic reasoning:** Men bear the full financial obligation of providing for their wives and children a woman's inherited wealth is entirely her own with no obligation to share it. Given this asymmetry of financial responsibility, the son's double share compensates for his greater financial obligations.

**The Wasiyyah (Will):** A Muslim may bequeath up to 1/3 of their estate to non-heirs (charities, friends, etc.) no more. The remaining 2/3+ is distributed according to Quranic shares.

## Key Takeaways

- The Nikah requires Offer and Acceptance, Wali (in Shafi'i/Hanbali), two witnesses, and Mahr.
- The Sunnah method of Talaq is one pronouncement followed by an 'Iddah during which reconciliation is possible.
- The wife has the right to Khul' returning the Mahr in exchange for divorce.
- The 'Iddah serves multiple purposes: confirming paternity, allowing reconciliation, and honoring the marriage's solemnity.
- Custody priority goes to the mother for young children; guardianship remains with the father.
- Islamic inheritance follows Quranic fixed shares the son's double portion reflects his greater financial obligations.
