## Islamic Economic Ethics

Islam does not separate business from ethics commercial activity is an act of worship when conducted according to divine principles. The Prophet was himself a merchant for 15 years before prophethood, known for his absolute trustworthiness.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Tirmidhi (Hasan)**
_"The truthful, trustworthy merchant will be with the Prophets, the truthful, and the martyrs."_
{% endhint %}

## Prohibited Elements in Contracts

### 1. Riba (Interest)

All forms of predetermined increase on loans categorically prohibited. See [Islamic Finance](islamic-finance.md) for full treatment.

### 2. Gharar (Excessive Uncertainty)

Transactions where the subject matter is fundamentally uncertain forbidden.

**Examples of Gharar:**

- Selling fish not yet caught
- Selling the unborn offspring of an animal (before knowing the offspring exists)
- Futures contracts with excessive speculation

**Permitted uncertainty:** Minor uncertainty inherent in all trade is permitted the prohibition applies to _excessive_ or _fundamental_ uncertainty.

### 3. Maysir (Gambling)

Any transaction where one party's gain is purely another's loss through chance prohibited.

**Note:** Insurance involves elements of uncertainty Islamic scholars have developed _Takaful_ (mutual guarantee) as a Sharia-compliant alternative.

### 4. Exploitation and Deception

- False advertising
- Hiding defects in goods being sold
- Cheating in weights and measures
- Price manipulation through false rumor

## Core Principles of Valid Islamic Contracts

| Principle                     | Requirement                                         |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Offer and Acceptance**      | Both parties must clearly agree                     |
| **Competency**                | Both parties must be sane, adult, and acting freely |
| **Subject matter must exist** | Cannot sell what you don't have                     |
| **Lawful subject matter**     | Cannot sell prohibited items (alcohol, pork, etc.)  |
| **Price must be known**       | Ambiguous price invalidates the contract            |

## The Islamic View of Profit

**Profit is halal** trade and commerce are explicitly permitted and praised in the Quran. The prohibition is not on profit but on _guaranteed profit without risk_ (Riba) and profit through deception or harm.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Baqarah 2:275**
**Translation:** "...And Allah has permitted trade and has forbidden Riba..."
{% endhint %}
