## Among the Prophet's Companions

The entire first generation of Muslims were reverts — they came from polytheism, and in some cases Christianity or Judaism, and entered Islam. Their stories are the original revert accounts.

Salman al-Farisi (RA) was a Persian Zoroastrian who had traveled from Persia to Syria to Iraq seeking a prophet he had been told was coming. Each Christian teacher he studied under directed him further east. When he heard of Muhammad in Madinah, he traveled there — and recognizing the prophetic signs he had been told to look for, accepted Islam immediately. The Prophet honored him by saying "Salman is from us, from the People of the House."

Abdullah ibn Salam (RA) was a Jewish rabbi in Madinah who recognized the Prophet's description matching what he had read in the Torah. His conversion was so significant that Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA) said: "When I heard of Abdullah ibn Salam's acceptance of Islam, I was more certain of the Prophet's truthfulness."

Negus (Ashama ibn Abjar) — the Christian King of Abyssinia who, after hearing Jafar ibn Abi Talib recite from Surah Maryam, wept and declared agreement with the Islamic account of Jesus. Many classical scholars consider him to have died as a Muslim. The Prophet prayed his funeral prayer in Madinah on the day he died in Abyssinia — an event witnessed by the Companions though separated by hundreds of miles.

Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA) — one of the most dramatic conversions in Islamic history. He had been among Islam's fiercest opponents, had planned to kill the Prophet, and was turned when he encountered the Quran being recited at his sister's home. His conversion was so significant that it immediately emboldened the Muslim community to pray publicly.
