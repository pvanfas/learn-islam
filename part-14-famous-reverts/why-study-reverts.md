## The Value of Revert Stories

The stories of people who come to Islam from outside the tradition serve multiple purposes for different audiences.

For non-Muslims, they demonstrate that Islam is intellectually and spiritually credible to people from diverse backgrounds who approached it critically rather than inheriting it by birth. A former atheist philosopher, a Christian theologian, or a secular scientist who becomes Muslim after serious investigation carries a different kind of testimony than someone born into the faith.

For born Muslims, reverts often see aspects of the religion with fresh eyes that born Muslims take for granted. Their accounts of what first drew them to Islam frequently illuminate what is most essential and most beautiful about the deen. Reading Hamza Yusuf's account of what struck him about Islamic theology, or Jeffrey Lang's encounter with the Quran, can renew a born Muslim's appreciation for what they have always had.

For reverts themselves, knowing that thousands of people from their background have made the same journey — and navigated its challenges — is a source of strength. The isolation that many reverts feel when their family and social world does not understand their choice is significantly eased by knowing they are part of a long and distinguished tradition of seekers who found their way to Islam.

For the Muslim community overall, reverts represent the continuing vitality of the da'wah obligation. The Prophet's words — "convey from me even a single verse" — are fulfilled whenever a Muslim's character, honesty, or spiritual depth inspires someone outside the faith to inquire further.
