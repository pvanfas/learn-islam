# Islam and the Environment Khalifah and Environmental Ethics

## In This Chapter

> Islam has a comprehensive environmental ethic embedded in its theology, law, and practice rooted in the concept of _Khalifah_ (stewardship). This chapter explores the Islamic relationship with the natural world, the specific Quranic and prophetic guidance on environmental care, and how these principles apply today.

---

## The Foundation Khalifah (Stewardship)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Baqarah 2:30**

**Translation:** "And when your Lord said to the angels: 'Indeed, I will make upon the earth a _Khalifah_ (steward/vicegerent)...'"

_The word Khalifah means steward or vicegerent one who manages something on behalf of another. Human beings are not the owners of the earth they are its trustees, accountable to the real Owner (Allah) for how they managed it._
{% endhint %}

The entire Islamic environmental ethic flows from this one verse. Humans are:

- **Not owners** of the earth they are temporary custodians
- **Accountable** to Allah for how they used the trust
- **Responsible** for those who come after them the earth is borrowed from future generations

## The Quran on Nature

The Quran does not treat the natural world as a resource to be exploited it treats it as a sign (_Ayah_) of Allah and a community of worshippers:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Isra 17:44**

**Translation:** "The seven heavens and the earth and all that is therein praise Him there is not a thing but glorifies His praise but you do not understand their glorification. Indeed, He is Forbearing and Forgiving."

_Every tree, every river, every creature is engaged in divine worship. Destroying them without necessity is interrupting divine praise._
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-An'am 6:38**

**Translation:** "And there is no creature on earth nor bird that flies with its wings except that they are communities like you..."

*Animals and creatures are described as *Umam* communities. They are not merely resources they are organized societies with their own existence before Allah.*
{% endhint %}

## The Prophetic Environmental Ethic

The Prophet gave specific guidance on environmental care that was extraordinary for 7th-century Arabia:

### Planting Trees

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih al-Bukhari and Muslim**

_"If the Hour (Day of Judgement) is about to be established and one of you was holding a palm shoot let him take advantage of even one second before the Hour is established to plant it."_

_Plant a tree even if the world is about to end. The act of creation and environmental contribution is intrinsically valuable._
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih Muslim**

*"If a Muslim plants a tree or sows seeds, and then a bird, or a person, or an animal eats from it it is regarded as a charitable gift (*Sadaqah*) for him."*

_Planting a tree is Sadaqah Jariyah ongoing charity. Every creature that benefits from it generates reward._
{% endhint %}

### Prohibition of Waste (_Israf_)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-A'raf 7:31**

**Translation:** "...and eat and drink and do not commit excess indeed, He does not like those who commit excess."
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sunan Ibn Majah (Sahih)**

The Prophet passed by Sa'd when he was performing Wudu and said: _"What is this waste?" Sa'd asked: "Can there be waste in Wudu?" He said: "Yes even if you are by a flowing river."_

_Even using water from a flowing river in excess is considered waste. The Prophet 's own Wudu used a minimal amount of water._
{% endhint %}

### Protection of Animals

The Prophet forbade:

- Striking animals on the face
- Branding animals on the face
- Keeping animals caged unnecessarily
- Killing animals for sport without need
- Making animals fight for entertainment

He narrated that a woman entered Hell for keeping a cat caged until it died and that a man entered Paradise for giving a thirsty dog water from his shoe. The moral weight of our treatment of animals is real in Islamic theology.

### Prohibition of Pollution

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih Muslim**

The Prophet said: _"Beware of the three acts that cause others to curse you: relieving yourselves in water sources, on the side of roads, and in the shade where people rest."_

_1,400 years before modern pollution law, the Prophet forbade contaminating water sources._
{% endhint %}

### The Protected Zones _Hima_ and _Haram_

The Prophet established two types of environmental protected zones:

**Hima** protected pastureland zones around settlements, where grazing was restricted to prevent overuse of land.

**Haram** sacred zones around Makkah and Madinah where cutting trees, killing animals, and disturbing the natural environment was prohibited.

These are among the earliest documented conservation zones in human history established 14 centuries ago.

## Islamic Environmental Principles for Today

### 1. No Waste (_La Israf_)

Overconsumption is not merely an economic or environmental issue it is a spiritual one. The Quran places _Mubaddhirin_ (wasteful people) alongside the companions of Shaytan:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Isra 17:2627**

**Translation:** "...And do not waste excessively. Indeed, the wasteful are brothers of the devils, and ever has Satan been to his Lord ungrateful."
{% endhint %}

**Application today:**

- Reduce single-use plastics
- Avoid food waste the Prophet would lick his fingers and the vessel clean before washing
- Buy what you need, not what advertising tells you to want
- Repair rather than replace where possible

### 2. Balance (_Mizan_)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Ar-Rahman 55:79**

**Translation:** "And the heaven He raised it and imposed the balance. That you not transgress within the balance. And establish weight in justice and do not make deficient the balance."
{% endhint %}

Allah created the universe in balance our responsibility is not to disturb that balance. Climate change, deforestation, ocean pollution, and species extinction are all disruptions of the _Mizan_ that Allah established.

### 3. Khalifah Accountability

Every Muslim will be asked about the earth they were given stewardship over. The Prophet said: _"Every one of you is a shepherd and every one of you is responsible for his flock."_ This extends to the natural world entrusted to us.

## The Muslim Environmental Action

**Individual:**

- Minimize waste in water, food, and consumption
- Plant trees even a single tree carries ongoing Sadaqah
- Treat animals with mercy their suffering is recorded
- Be mindful of your carbon footprint as a form of not harming others

**Community:**

- Mosques can model sustainable practices (solar panels, water conservation, eliminating single-use items)
- Islamic organizations can participate in environmental advocacy
- Environmental stewardship can be a powerful form of da'wah showing that Islamic values align with human wellbeing

---

## Key Takeaways

- _Khalifah_ (stewardship) is the foundation of Islamic environmental ethics humans are trustees, not owners, of the earth.
- The Quran treats nature as a community of worshippers, not merely a human resource.
- The Prophet established specific environmental protections against waste of water, pollution of sources, cruelty to animals, and unnecessary cutting of trees.
- Planting a tree is Sadaqah Jariyah every creature that benefits generates ongoing reward.
- Waste (_Israf_) is associated with the companions of Shaytan in the Quran.
- Islamic environmental ethics rooted in Khalifah, Mizan, and the prohibition of Israf provide a comprehensive theological basis for environmental responsibility.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<details>

<summary>Does Islam address climate change specifically?</summary>

Not by name but the principles are clear. Altering the earth's climate in ways that harm billions of people violates the prohibition on harm (_La darar wa la dirar_ no harm and no reciprocal harm, a foundational Islamic legal maxim). Causing environmental damage that harms future generations violates the principle of stewardship (_Khalifah_). And the disruption of Allah's created balance (_Mizan_) is explicitly warned against in the Quran. Contemporary Islamic scholars have increasingly engaged with climate change as an Islamic concern.

</details>
