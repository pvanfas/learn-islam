# Islam and the Environment — Khalifah and Environmental Ethics

**Difficulty:** 🟢 Beginner &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~9 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 17 — Living as a Muslim

---

## 📌 In This Chapter

> Islam has a comprehensive environmental ethic embedded in its theology, law, and practice — rooted in the concept of *Khalifah* (stewardship). This chapter explores the Islamic relationship with the natural world, the specific Quranic and prophetic guidance on environmental care, and how these principles apply today.

---

## 📖 The Foundation — Khalifah (Stewardship)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Baqarah 2:30**

**Translation:** "And when your Lord said to the angels: 'Indeed, I will make upon the earth a *Khalifah* (steward/vicegerent)...'"

*The word Khalifah means steward or vicegerent — one who manages something on behalf of another. Human beings are not the owners of the earth — they are its trustees, accountable to the real Owner (Allah) for how they managed it.*
{% endhint %}

The entire Islamic environmental ethic flows from this one verse. Humans are:
- **Not owners** of the earth — they are temporary custodians
- **Accountable** to Allah for how they used the trust
- **Responsible** for those who come after them — the earth is borrowed from future generations

---

## 🌱 The Quran on Nature

The Quran does not treat the natural world as a resource to be exploited — it treats it as a sign (*Ayah*) of Allah and a community of worshippers:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Isra 17:44**

**Translation:** "The seven heavens and the earth and all that is therein praise Him — there is not a thing but glorifies His praise — but you do not understand their glorification. Indeed, He is Forbearing and Forgiving."

*Every tree, every river, every creature is engaged in divine worship. Destroying them without necessity is interrupting divine praise.*
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-An'am 6:38**

**Translation:** "And there is no creature on earth nor bird that flies with its wings except that they are communities like you..."

*Animals and creatures are described as *Umam* — communities. They are not merely resources — they are organized societies with their own existence before Allah.*
{% endhint %}

## 🌊 The Prophetic Environmental Ethic

The Prophet ﷺ gave specific guidance on environmental care that was extraordinary for 7th-century Arabia:

### Planting Trees

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sahih al-Bukhari and Muslim**

*"If the Hour (Day of Judgement) is about to be established and one of you was holding a palm shoot — let him take advantage of even one second before the Hour is established to plant it."*

*Plant a tree even if the world is about to end. The act of creation and environmental contribution is intrinsically valuable.*
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sahih Muslim**

*"If a Muslim plants a tree or sows seeds, and then a bird, or a person, or an animal eats from it — it is regarded as a charitable gift (*Sadaqah*) for him."*

*Planting a tree is Sadaqah Jariyah — ongoing charity. Every creature that benefits from it generates reward.*
{% endhint %}

### Prohibition of Waste (*Israf*)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-A'raf 7:31**

**Translation:** "...and eat and drink and do not commit excess — indeed, He does not like those who commit excess."
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sunan Ibn Majah (Sahih)**

The Prophet ﷺ passed by Sa'd when he was performing Wudu and said: *"What is this waste?" Sa'd asked: "Can there be waste in Wudu?" He said: "Yes — even if you are by a flowing river."*

*Even using water from a flowing river in excess is considered waste. The Prophet ﷺ's own Wudu used a minimal amount of water.*
{% endhint %}

### Protection of Animals

The Prophet ﷺ forbade:
- Striking animals on the face
- Branding animals on the face
- Keeping animals caged unnecessarily
- Killing animals for sport without need
- Making animals fight for entertainment

He narrated that a woman entered Hell for keeping a cat caged until it died — and that a man entered Paradise for giving a thirsty dog water from his shoe. The moral weight of our treatment of animals is real in Islamic theology.

### Prohibition of Pollution

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sahih Muslim**

The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Beware of the three acts that cause others to curse you: relieving yourselves in water sources, on the side of roads, and in the shade where people rest."*

*1,400 years before modern pollution law, the Prophet ﷺ forbade contaminating water sources.*
{% endhint %}

### The Protected Zones — *Hima* and *Haram*

The Prophet ﷺ established two types of environmental protected zones:

**Hima** — protected pastureland zones around settlements, where grazing was restricted to prevent overuse of land.

**Haram** — sacred zones around Makkah and Madinah where cutting trees, killing animals, and disturbing the natural environment was prohibited.

These are among the earliest documented conservation zones in human history — established 14 centuries ago.

## 🌍 Islamic Environmental Principles for Today

### 1 — No Waste (*La Israf*)

Overconsumption is not merely an economic or environmental issue — it is a spiritual one. The Quran places *Mubaddhirin* (wasteful people) alongside the companions of Shaytan:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Isra 17:26–27**

**Translation:** "...And do not waste excessively. Indeed, the wasteful are brothers of the devils, and ever has Satan been to his Lord ungrateful."
{% endhint %}

**Application today:**
- Reduce single-use plastics
- Avoid food waste — the Prophet ﷺ would lick his fingers and the vessel clean before washing
- Buy what you need, not what advertising tells you to want
- Repair rather than replace where possible

### 2 — Balance (*Mizan*)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Ar-Rahman 55:7–9**

**Translation:** "And the heaven He raised it and imposed the balance. That you not transgress within the balance. And establish weight in justice and do not make deficient the balance."
{% endhint %}

Allah created the universe in balance — our responsibility is not to disturb that balance. Climate change, deforestation, ocean pollution, and species extinction are all disruptions of the *Mizan* that Allah established.

### 3 — Khalifah Accountability

Every Muslim will be asked about the earth they were given stewardship over. The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Every one of you is a shepherd and every one of you is responsible for his flock."* This extends to the natural world entrusted to us.

## 🤲 The Muslim Environmental Action

**Individual:**
- Minimize waste in water, food, and consumption
- Plant trees — even a single tree carries ongoing Sadaqah
- Treat animals with mercy — their suffering is recorded
- Be mindful of your carbon footprint as a form of not harming others

**Community:**
- Mosques can model sustainable practices (solar panels, water conservation, eliminating single-use items)
- Islamic organizations can participate in environmental advocacy
- Environmental stewardship can be a powerful form of da'wah — showing that Islamic values align with human wellbeing

---

## ✅ Key Takeaways

- *Khalifah* (stewardship) is the foundation of Islamic environmental ethics — humans are trustees, not owners, of the earth.
- The Quran treats nature as a community of worshippers, not merely a human resource.
- The Prophet ﷺ established specific environmental protections — against waste of water, pollution of sources, cruelty to animals, and unnecessary cutting of trees.
- Planting a tree is Sadaqah Jariyah — every creature that benefits generates ongoing reward.
- Waste (*Israf*) is associated with the companions of Shaytan in the Quran.
- Islamic environmental ethics — rooted in Khalifah, Mizan, and the prohibition of Israf — provide a comprehensive theological basis for environmental responsibility.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

<details>

<summary>Does Islam address climate change specifically?</summary>

Not by name — but the principles are clear. Altering the earth's climate in ways that harm billions of people violates the prohibition on harm (*La darar wa la dirar* — no harm and no reciprocal harm, a foundational Islamic legal maxim). Causing environmental damage that harms future generations violates the principle of stewardship (*Khalifah*). And the disruption of Allah's created balance (*Mizan*) is explicitly warned against in the Quran. Contemporary Islamic scholars have increasingly engaged with climate change as an Islamic concern.

</details>
