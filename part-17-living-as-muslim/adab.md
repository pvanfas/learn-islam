# Islamic Manners (_Adab_) in Daily Life

**Difficulty:** Beginner &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~12 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 17 — Living as a Muslim

---

## In This Chapter

> _Adab_ — Islamic manners and etiquette — is one of the most defining characteristics of a Muslim's daily life. It covers how a Muslim greets others, eats, sleeps, enters and leaves spaces, speaks, dresses, and relates to people of all kinds. This chapter covers the core Adab of daily life drawn from the Sunnah — practical, doable, and transformative.

## Foundation of Adab

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Musnad Ahmad (Sahih)**

The Prophet ﷺ said: _"I was sent to perfect good character."_

_Adab is not a collection of customs. It is the practical expression of faith — the outward manifestation of the inner state of Iman. A Muslim with beautiful manners is a walking da'wah._
{% endhint %}

Imam Malik (RA) said to his student: _"Learn Adab before you learn knowledge."_

The scholars of the early generations understood that Adab precedes knowledge, because knowledge without Adab produces arrogance — while Adab without knowledge produces sincere striving.

## The Greeting — _As-Salamu Alaykum_

The Islamic greeting is not merely a social nicety — it is an act of worship with reward, a declaration of safety, and a dua (supplication) for the person you are greeting.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sahih Muslim**

The Prophet ﷺ said: _"You will not enter Paradise until you believe, and you will not believe until you love one another. Shall I not tell you something that, if you do it, you will love one another? Spread the Salam among yourselves."_
{% endhint %}

**The levels of Salam:**

| Form   | Arabic                                           | Meaning                             | Minimum reward |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | -------------- |
| Short  | _As-salamu alaykum_                              | Peace be upon you                   | 10 good deeds  |
| Medium | _As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullah_               | Peace and Allah's mercy be upon you | 20 good deeds  |
| Full   | _As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullahi wa barakatuh_ | Peace, mercy, and blessings         | 30 good deeds  |

**Key rules of Salam:**

- The one arriving greets first
- The younger greets the older; the walking greets the seated; the small group greets the larger
- A Muslim must respond to Salam with at least the same or better
- Begin with Salam before speaking — the Prophet ﷺ said: _"Do not allow anyone to begin speaking before the Salam"_ (Tirmidhi)

## Eating and Drinking Sunnah

The Sunnah of eating transforms a biological act into worship:

**Before eating:**

- Say _Bismillah_ — if forgotten, say when you remember: _"Bismillahi awwalahu wa akhirah"_
- Wash hands before and after
- Eat with the **right hand**
- Eat from what is nearest to you on the plate — not from the center

**During eating:**

- Eat together — the Prophet ﷺ said food for one suffices two, food for two suffices four
- Do not criticize food — if you dislike something, leave it without comment
- Do not blow on hot food — wait for it to cool
- Do not eat lying flat on the back
- Sit to eat — the Prophet ﷺ ate sitting on the floor, not reclining against a support while eating

**After eating:**

{% hint style="info" %}
**الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَطْعَمَنَا وَسَقَانَا وَجَعَلَنَا مُسْلِمِين**

**Transliteration:** _Alhamdu lillahil-ladhi at'amana wa saqana wa ja'alana Muslimin_

**Translation:** "All praise is for Allah who fed us and gave us drink and made us Muslims."

_(Abu Dawud — Sahih)_
{% endhint %}

**Drinking:**

- Drink with the right hand
- Do not drink standing (Makruh — disliked — though not forbidden in the Shafi'i position)
- Do not breathe into a vessel — breathe outside the cup
- Drink in three sips, taking breath outside the cup between them

## Entering and Leaving the Home

**Before entering:**

- Say _Bismillah_ — the Prophet ﷺ said this prevents Shaytan from spending the night in your home
- Say _Salam_ upon entering — even if no one is home

{% hint style="info" %}
**اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْأَلُكَ خَيْرَ الْمَوْلِجِ وَخَيْرَ الْمَخْرَجِ، بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَلَجْنَا، وَبِسْمِ اللَّهِ خَرَجْنَا، وَعَلَى اللَّهِ رَبِّنَا تَوَكَّلْنَا**

**Transliteration:** _Allahumma inni as'aluka khayral-mawliji wa khayral-makhraji, bismillahi walajna wa bismillahi kharajna wa 'ala Allahi rabbina tawakkalna_

**Translation:** "O Allah, I ask You for the best entry and the best exit. In the name of Allah we enter, in the name of Allah we exit, and in Allah our Lord we place our trust."

_(Abu Dawud — Hasan)_
{% endhint %}

**Entering a mosque:**

- Enter with the right foot
- Say the dua for entering the mosque: _"Allahumma iftah li abwaba rahmatik"_ — "O Allah, open for me the doors of Your mercy"
- Leave with the left foot
- Say the dua for leaving: _"Allahumma inni as'aluka min fadlik"_ — "O Allah, I ask You of Your bounty"

## The Sunnah of Sleep

Sleep is treated in the Sunnah as a minor death — and waking from it as a resurrection. The bedtime routine reflects this:

**Before sleeping:**

1. Perform Wudu (recommended)
2. Sleep on the **right side** — facing the Qiblah
3. Place the right hand under the right cheek
4. Recite Ayat al-Kursi — protection through the night
5. Recite Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq, An-Nas — blow into palms, wipe over body (×3)
6. Recite: _"Subhanallah"_ (×33), _"Alhamdulillah"_ (×33), _"Allahu Akbar"_ (×34)
7. Say the sleeping dua: _"Bismika Allahumma amutu wa ahya"_ — "In Your name, O Allah, I die and I live"

**Upon waking:**

- Say: _"Alhamdulillahil-ladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana wa ilayhin-nushur"_ — "All praise is for Allah who gave us life after causing us to die, and to Him is the return"
- Begin Wudu for Fajr

## The Adab of Speech

The Prophet ﷺ gave the foundation principle:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sahih al-Bukhari and Muslim**

_"Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day, let him say good or remain silent."_
{% endhint %}

**Specific Adab of speech:**

| Situation                        | Adab                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Sneezing**                     | Say _"Alhamdulillah"_. Those present respond: _"Yarhamukallah"_ (Allah have mercy on you). Sneezer replies: _"Yahdikumullahu wa yuslihu balakum"_ |
| **Yawning**                      | Cover the mouth. Do not make sound. The Prophet ﷺ said yawning is from Shaytan — suppress it                                                      |
| **Entering conversation**        | Begin with Salam, not with a request                                                                                                              |
| **Lying**                        | Strictly prohibited — even in jest                                                                                                                |
| **Backbiting (_Ghibah_)**        | Mentioning about a person what they would dislike, even if true — strictly forbidden (Surah Al-Hujurat 49:12)                                     |
| **Tale-carrying (_Namimah_)**    | Spreading words between people to cause discord — among the greatest sins                                                                         |
| **Swearing by other than Allah** | Prohibited — only swear by Allah                                                                                                                  |
| **Saying _Inshallah_**           | Said for future intentions — not sarcastically                                                                                                    |

## The Adab of Dress and Appearance

**General principles:**

- Begin dressing with the **right side** (right sleeve, right shoe first)
- Remove beginning with the **left side**
- Say _Bismillah_ when dressing
- Do not wear clothing that is prideful or arrogant in intent

**When putting on new clothing:**

{% hint style="info" %}
_اللَّهُمَّ لَكَ الْحَمْدُ أَنْتَ كَسَوْتَنِيهِ، أَسْأَلُكَ مِنْ خَيْرِهِ وَخَيْرِ مَا صُنِعَ لَهُ، وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ شَرِّهِ وَشَرِّ مَا صُنِعَ لَهُ_

**Translation:** "O Allah, all praise is Yours — You have clothed me with it. I ask You of its good and the good of what it was made for, and I seek refuge in You from its evil and the evil of what it was made for."

_(Abu Dawud — Hasan)_
{% endhint %}

## The Adab of Travel

**Before travelling:**

- Bid family farewell
- Two Rak'ahs of travel prayer (Sunnah)
- Dua when departing

{% hint style="info" %}
**سُبْحَانَ الَّذِي سَخَّرَ لَنَا هَٰذَا وَمَا كُنَّا لَهُ مُقْرِنِينَ وَإِنَّا إِلَىٰ رَبِّنَا لَمُنقَلِبُونَ**

**Transliteration:** _Subhanal-ladhi sakhkhara lana hadha wa ma kunna lahu muqrinin, wa inna ila rabbina lamunqalibun_

**Translation:** "Glory be to the One who has subjected this to us, for we could not have done it ourselves. And indeed, to our Lord we shall return."

_(Surah Az-Zukhruf 43:13–14 — recited when boarding any vehicle)_
{% endhint %}

- When ascending (a hill, plane taking off): say _"Allahu Akbar"_
- When descending: say _"Subhanallah"_

## Respect for Elders — A Pillar of Islamic Social Adab

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sunan Abu Dawud (Sahih)**

_"It is not from us — he who does not show mercy to our young and does not show respect to our elders."_
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sahih al-Bukhari**

_"Respecting an old person is from the glorification of Allah."_
{% endhint %}

- Stand for an elder entering the room
- Give them precedence in speaking, eating, and passing through doors
- Do not interrupt or dismiss the experience of those older
- Give the right of reply first to elders in conversation

## Rights of Neighbors

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sahih al-Bukhari and Muslim**

_"Jibreel kept advising me about the neighbor until I thought he would be made an heir."_
{% endhint %}

The Prophet ﷺ defined "the neighbor" as extending **40 houses in every direction**. The rights of neighbors include:

- Not harming them or their property
- Sharing food when you cook — sending some to them
- Visiting them in illness
- Supporting them in difficulty
- Maintaining good relations regardless of their religion or background

The Prophet ﷺ said a person whose neighbor is not safe from their harm has not entered true faith.

---

## Key Takeaways

- _Adab_ — Islamic manners — is the practical expression of faith in every moment of daily life.
- The Islamic greeting (_Salam_) is an act of worship, a dua, and a key to community love.
- Eating, drinking, sleeping, entering and leaving, speaking, and traveling all have Sunnah that transforms ordinary moments into worship.
- The right hand is used for honorable acts; the left for cleansing.
- Backbiting (_Ghibah_) and tale-carrying (_Namimah_) are among the most socially destructive sins — both explicitly forbidden.
- Respect for elders and rights of neighbors are cornerstones of Islamic social life.
