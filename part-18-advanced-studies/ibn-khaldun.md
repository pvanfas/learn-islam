## The Most Original Mind in Islamic Intellectual History

Abd al-Rahman ibn Khaldun (732-808 AH / 1332-1406 CE) is arguably the most intellectually original figure produced by Islamic civilization. He is widely recognized as the founder of sociology, the philosophy of history, and in important respects modern historiography.

Arnold Toynbee called his Muqaddimah "undoubtedly the greatest work of its kind that has ever yet been created by any mind in any time or place."

---

## The Muqaddimah

The "Muqaddimah" (Introduction) was written as the preface to his massive history "Kitab al-Ibar" but it became far more important than the history it introduced. Written in approximately five months while Ibn Khaldun was in isolation in a small fort in the Atlas Mountains of Algeria, it contains what he called a new science: the science of human civilization (ilm al-umran).

---

## The Central Theory Asabiyyah and the Cycle of Civilizations

Ibn Khaldun's most original contribution is his theory of asabiyyah group solidarity, social cohesion, or what we might call collective will. His central argument:

Nomadic and tribal peoples develop strong asabiyyah through the harsh conditions of desert and frontier life mutual dependence, solidarity against external threats, physical hardness. This strong asabiyyah gives them the capacity to conquer settled civilizations that have grown soft.

When they conquer and establish a dynasty, they begin to enjoy the luxury and ease of urban life. Over generations, asabiyyah weakens luxury softens, distinctions emerge, individuals pursue personal interest over collective solidarity.

After approximately three to four generations (roughly 120 years), the dynasty has so weakened in asabiyyah that a new group from the periphery hungry, hard, and cohesive overthrows it. The cycle repeats.

This theory explained, with remarkable precision, the rise and fall of the dynasties Ibn Khaldun had observed throughout Islamic history the Almoravids, Almohads, Fatimids, and the recurring pattern of tribal dynasties replacing urban ones.

---

## Ibn Khaldun's Historiographical Revolution

Before Ibn Khaldun, Islamic historians simply narrated events transmitting accounts without systematic analysis of causation or pattern. Ibn Khaldun insisted that history must be explanatory: understanding why events happen, not merely recording that they did.

He was deeply skeptical of inherited historical accounts not because he doubted the transmission, but because he recognized that narrators embellish, that geographical and social conditions constrain what is possible, and that miraculous accounts require exceptional evidence. His criteria for evaluating historical claims anticipated the principles of modern historical criticism.

---

## Ibn Khaldun and the Contemporary World

Ibn Khaldun's theory has been applied to modern geopolitics scholars have used it to analyze the decline of established powers and the rise of new ones, the relationship between frontier and urban culture, and the sociology of political power.

His observations about the economics of taxation ("the state that reduces taxes generates more revenue in the long run than the state that raises them" a principle anticipating the Laffer Curve by 600 years) show the range of his economic insight.
