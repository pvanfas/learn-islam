## Mathematics

**Al-Khwarizmi** (780-850 CE) _Kitab al-Mukhtasar fi Hisab al-Jabr wal-Muqabala_

The word **algebra** comes directly from _Al-Jabr_ in the title of this book the systematic treatment of linear and quadratic equations. Al-Khwarizmi created the foundation of modern mathematics.

The word **algorithm** comes from the Latinization of his name _Algoritmi_ because his works on calculation were so foundational that European mathematicians named the process of calculation after him.

He also championed the Indian numeral system (the digits 09 we use today) as superior to Roman numerals and this transmission through Arabic texts is why these are called _Arabic numerals_.

**Al-Battani** (858-929 CE) refined trigonometric functions, corrected astronomical calculations by Ptolemy, and calculated the solar year with remarkable precision.

**Omar Khayyam** (1048-1131 CE) solved cubic equations geometrically, developed a solar calendar more accurate than the Gregorian calendar (which came 500 years later), and his calendar is still used in Iran.

## Physics and Optics

**Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen)** (965-1040 CE) _Kitab al-Manazir_ (Book of Optics)

Ibn al-Haytham's _Book of Optics_ written while he was under house arrest in Egypt after falsely claiming he could regulate the Nile is one of the most influential scientific texts in history. It was the primary text on optics in Europe for 600 years after its translation into Latin.

His contributions:

- Correctly described how vision works (light from objects enters the eye not the Greek belief that rays emit from the eye)
- Described the camera obscura
- Performed the first controlled scientific experiments with independent variables
- Founded the scientific method of empirical testing before Ibn al-Haytham was fully recognized in Europe

**Al-Biruni** (973-1048 CE):

- Calculated the Earth's radius to within 1% of the modern value using his own mathematical method
- Described the Earth as rotating on its axis
- Developed methods for determining latitude and longitude
- Made the first systematic anthropological study of Indian civilization

## Medicine

**Ibn Sina (Avicenna)** (980-1037 CE) _Al-Qanun fi al-Tibb_ (Canon of Medicine)

Ibn Sina's _Canon of Medicine_ a million-word encyclopedia of all medical knowledge was the standard medical textbook in European universities until the 17th century. It was translated into Latin and used at Oxford, Montpellier, Padua, and Louvain.

His contributions:

- Described the contagious nature of disease proposing quarantine 600 years before Europe understood germ theory
- Identified tuberculosis as a contagious disease
- Described the role of soil and water in disease transmission
- Developed drug testing protocols
- Discovered that mental illness is a medical condition

**Ibn al-Nafis** (1213-1288 CE):

- Correctly described pulmonary circulation blood circulating through the lungs 300 years before William Harvey's famous discovery

**The first hospitals:**
The first hospital in the modern sense providing free medical care to all, with separate wards by condition, medical records, and trained staff was the **Bimaristan** established in Baghdad under the Abbasid Caliphate. By the 10th century, Baghdad had 860 licensed physicians. Medieval Europe had no equivalent institution.

## Geography and Navigation

**Al-Idrisi** (1100-1165 CE):
Commissioned by the Norman King Roger II of Sicily, Al-Idrisi produced the most accurate world map of the medieval period the _Tabula Rogeriana_. He divided the world into seven climate zones and compiled geographical data more comprehensive than anything before it.

**Islamic navigational contributions:**

- The **magnetic compass** was refined and popularized through Arabic texts
- Astronomical navigation using star positions for maritime navigation
- The **astrolabe** was refined by Islamic astronomers and used for prayer timing, navigation, and astronomy

## Coffee and Social Culture

**Coffee** (_Qahwah_) was first cultivated and consumed in Yemen, likely by Sufi orders who used it to stay awake for night prayers. From Yemen, coffee spread to Cairo, Istanbul, and then to Europe where the first coffeehouses in London and Paris were established by Arab and Ottoman merchants. The coffeehouse culture of intellectual discussion that transformed European Enlightenment culture was a direct inheritance from Islamic civilization's _Qahwah Khana_.

## Architecture

**The Great Mosque of Córdoba** (785-987 CE) one of the most beautiful buildings in human history; its hypostyle hall of 856 columns influenced cathedral architecture across Europe.

**The Alhambra Palace** (Granada, Spain, 13th–14th century) a masterwork of Islamic geometric art, muqarnas (honeycomb vaulting), calligraphic decoration, and the integration of water into architecture.

**The Dome of the Rock** (Jerusalem, 691 CE) one of the oldest extant Islamic buildings; its octagonal design and golden dome influenced Byzantine and later Christian church architecture.

**Pointed arch, ribbed vault, and flying buttress:**
All three structural innovations of Gothic cathedral architecture which define European medieval cathedrals were first developed or refined in Islamic architecture and transmitted to Europe during the Crusades.

## Education and the University

**Al-Qarawiyyin** (Fez, Morocco, 859 CE) founded by **Fatima al-Fihri**, a Muslim woman is recognized by UNESCO and Guinness World Records as the **oldest continuously operating university in the world**.

**Al-Azhar** (Cairo, Egypt, 970 CE) still the world's preeminent center of Islamic learning, founded by the Fatimid dynasty.

**The madrasa system** the structured institution of organized higher education, with a curriculum, formal degrees, and residential housing for students was the model on which European universities were built. The University of Bologna (1088 CE) was founded just a century after the Islamic madrasa system had fully developed.

## The Preservation of Antiquity

One of Islamic civilization's greatest gifts to humanity was the preservation of Greek philosophy and science. When the Western Roman Empire collapsed and Europe entered the Dark Ages, Greek texts were largely lost to Western European civilization. **Islamic scholars translated, commented upon, and extended** the works of Aristotle, Plato, Ptolemy, Galen, Euclid, and others.

When Europe's Renaissance began, it was built almost entirely on:

- Arabic translations of Greek texts (retranslated into Latin)
- Arabic _commentaries_ on those texts that went far beyond the originals
- Islamic _advances_ in mathematics, medicine, and optics

The Renaissance was, in significant part, the rediscovery of Greek knowledge through Arabic a debt that European historiography has been slow to acknowledge.

## Key Takeaways

- Islamic civilization's intellectual explosion was driven by a theological imperative seeking knowledge is worship.
- **Mathematics:** Al-Khwarizmi invented algebra and championed Arabic numerals the word algorithm comes from his name.
- **Physics:** Ibn al-Haytham's _Book of Optics_ founded the scientific method and was Europe's primary optics text for 600 years.
- **Medicine:** Ibn Sina's _Canon of Medicine_ was Europe's standard medical textbook until the 17th century; Islamic hospitals were the first in the modern sense.
- **Geography:** Al-Idrisi produced the most accurate medieval world map; Islamic navigational tools enabled the Age of Exploration.
- **Architecture:** Pointed arch, ribbed vault, and honeycomb vaulting of Gothic cathedrals developed from Islamic architecture.
- **Education:** The world's oldest university (Al-Qarawiyyin) was founded by a Muslim woman; the madrasa system was the model for European universities.
- **The Renaissance itself** was largely built on Islamic preservation and advancement of Greek knowledge.
