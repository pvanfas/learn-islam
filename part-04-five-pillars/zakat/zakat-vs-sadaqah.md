# Zakat vs. Sadaqah Obligatory and Voluntary Charity

**Difficulty:** Beginner &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~9 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 4 Five Pillars Zakat

---

## In This Chapter

> Islam has two categories of charity Zakat, which is obligatory and precisely defined, and Sadaqah, which is voluntary and encompasses an extraordinarily broad range of acts. Understanding both their differences, their rewards, and how they complement each other transforms the Muslim's relationship with wealth and giving.

---

## Quranic Foundation

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Baqarah 2:177**

**Translation:** "Righteousness is not that you turn your faces toward the east or the west, but true righteousness is in one who believes in Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Book, and the prophets and gives wealth despite loving it to relatives, orphans, the needy, the traveler, those who ask, and for the freeing of slaves..."

_True righteousness is not ritual performance alone it is expressed concretely through giving._
{% endhint %}

---

## Key Differences Between Zakat and Sadaqah

| Feature                     | Zakat                                             | Sadaqah                                                      |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Status**                  | Obligatory (_Fard_)                               | Voluntary (_Nafl_)                                           |
| **Amount**                  | Specified (2.5% of zakatable wealth)              | No limit give as much or as little as you wish               |
| **Recipients**              | Eight specified categories (Surah At-Tawbah 9:60) | Anyone in need Muslim or non-Muslim                          |
| **Timing**                  | Due after one full lunar year above Nisab         | Any time                                                     |
| **Consequence of omission** | Sinful a pillar of Islam abandoned                | No sin but significant missed reward                         |
| **Purpose**                 | Purification of wealth; fulfillment of obligation | Additional good; building character; relationship with Allah |

## Sadaqah The Vast World of Voluntary Charity

Sadaqah is one of the most expansive concepts in Islamic ethics. The Prophet described it in ways that make every Muslim a potential giver regardless of wealth:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih al-Bukhari**

_"Every joint of every person must perform a Sadaqah every day the sun rises. Bringing about justice between two people is Sadaqah. Helping a person onto their ride or lifting their luggage onto it is Sadaqah. A good word is Sadaqah. Every step toward the prayer is Sadaqah. Removing something harmful from the road is Sadaqah."_
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih al-Bukhari and Muslim**

_"Your smile in the face of your brother is Sadaqah."_
{% endhint %}

**Types of Sadaqah:**

| Type              | Example                                                                        |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Financial**     | Giving money to the poor, funding a school, donating food                      |
| **Time**          | Volunteering, teaching, helping someone in need                                |
| **Knowledge**     | Teaching Quran, Islamic knowledge, beneficial skills                           |
| **Physical help** | Carrying bags, removing obstacles from the road                                |
| **Emotional**     | A smile, a kind word, visiting the sick                                        |
| **Du'a**          | Praying for others a form of Sadaqah                                           |
| **Environmental** | Planting a tree (_"if the Hour comes while you are planting a tree plant it"_) |

---

## Sadaqah Jariyah The Ongoing Charity

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih Muslim**

*"When a person dies, all their actions come to an end except three: ongoing Sadaqah (*Sadaqah Jariyah*), knowledge that others benefit from, or a righteous child who prays for them."*
{% endhint %}

**Sadaqah Jariyah** (ongoing charity) is perhaps the most powerful investment a Muslim can make it continues to generate reward after death:

| Sadaqah Jariyah                       | How it continues                                     |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Building a mosque**                 | Every prayer prayed there earns reward for you       |
| **Digging a well**                    | Every drink taken earns reward                       |
| **Funding a school or madrasa**       | Every lesson learned earns reward                    |
| **Planting trees**                    | Every fruit eaten, every shade given earns reward    |
| **Teaching knowledge**                | Every time the student uses it or teaches it further |
| **Having righteous children**         | Their prayers continue for you                       |
| **Writing a beneficial book/article** | Every person who reads and benefits                  |

## The Extraordinary Reward of Sadaqah

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Baqarah 2:261**

**Translation:** "The example of those who spend their wealth in the way of Allah is like a seed which grows seven spikes in each spike is a hundred grains. Allah multiplies for whom He wills. And Allah is all-Encompassing and Knowing."

_700-fold reward for Sadaqah given sincerely. And the verse ends: "Allah multiplies for whom He wills" the actual multiplier may be far greater._
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih al-Bukhari**

_"Charity does not decrease wealth. No one forgives another except that Allah increases him in honor. And no one humbles himself for the sake of Allah except that Allah raises his status."_
{% endhint %}

## When to Give Sadaqah

**On Fridays:** The Prophet said Friday is the best day of the week and giving Sadaqah on Friday multiplies its reward. Many scholars particularly recommend it.

**In Ramadan:** The Prophet was _"the most generous of people, and he was at his most generous in Ramadan."_ Sadaqah in Ramadan is multiplied in reward.

**When tested:** Sadaqah as a response to hardship the Prophet said: _"Treat your sick with Sadaqah."_ Giving when it is difficult is more beloved to Allah.

**Secretly:** The highest form of Sadaqah keeps the giver hidden:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Baqarah 2:271**

**Translation:** "If you give alms openly, it is well; but if you hide it and give it to the poor, that is better for you and will expiate some of your sins. And Allah is Aware of what you do."
{% endhint %}

The Prophet mentioned seven people who will be under Allah's shade on the Day of Judgement one of them: _"A man who gives charity so secretly that his left hand does not know what his right hand has given."_

## Practical Giving Building a Habit

**Start small but consistent:**

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih al-Bukhari**

_"The most beloved deeds to Allah are those that are most consistent, even if they are small."_
{% endhint %}

**Suggested practices:**

- A daily Sadaqah jar even small coins daily
- Friday Sadaqah give something every Friday
- Ramadan giving plan calculate Zakat + increase Sadaqah throughout the month
- Sadaqah Jariyah fund save specifically for a well, school sponsorship, or mosque contribution
- _"Sadaqah first"_ rule when you receive income, give a portion before spending

---

## Key Takeaways

- Zakat is obligatory (2.5% above Nisab, eight Quranic recipients), Sadaqah is voluntary (unlimited, anyone in need).
- Sadaqah is vastly broader than financial giving a smile, a kind word, removing harm from the road are all Sadaqah.
- Sadaqah Jariyah (ongoing charity) continues to earn reward after death among the three things that don't stop with death.
- The reward of Sadaqah is multiplied up to 700 times or more and charity does not decrease wealth.
- Giving secretly is the highest form; Friday and Ramadan are optimal times; consistency is more beloved than quantity.
