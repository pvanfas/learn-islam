# Understanding Zakat The Obligatory Charity

**Difficulty:** Beginner &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~11 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 4 The Five Pillars Zakat

---

## In This Chapter

> Zakat is the third pillar of Islam an obligatory annual charity that purifies wealth and redistributes it to those in need. This chapter explains what Zakat is, the theological wisdom behind it, who must pay it, what assets it applies to, and the eight categories of people who are entitled to receive it.

## Quranic Foundation

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah At-Tawbah 9:103**

_خُذْ مِنْ أَمْوَالِهِمْ صَدَقَةً تُطَهِّرُهُمْ وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا_

**Translation:** "Take from their wealth a charity by which you purify them and cause them increase, and invoke [Allah's blessings] upon them. Indeed, your invocations are reassurance for them. And Allah is Hearing and Knowing."

_The verse itself reveals the dual meaning of Zakat: it purifies the giver and causes growth both spiritually and in blessing._
{% endhint %}

## What Is Zakat?

The word **Zakat** (زَكَاة) comes from two Arabic roots simultaneously _Zakaa_, meaning **purification**, and _Zakaa_, meaning **growth** and **blessing**. Both meanings are intentional: Zakat purifies wealth from spiritual attachment and entitlement, and it grows both the giver's reward and through Allah's blessing their wealth itself.

Zakat is mentioned in the Quran **82 times** almost always alongside the prayer (_Salah_):

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Baqarah 2:43**

**Translation:** "And establish prayer and give Zakat and bow with those who bow [in worship]."
{% endhint %}

This pairing is deeply intentional. Salah is the connection between the servant and Allah. Zakat is the connection between the Muslim and the community. A faith that is only vertical between you and God and has no horizontal expression in care for others is incomplete. Zakat institutionalizes that care.

## Who Must Pay Zakat?

Zakat is **obligatory (_Fard_)** upon every Muslim who meets all of the following conditions:

| Condition                          | Detail                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Muslim**                         | Zakat is an act of Islamic worship only obligatory upon Muslims                                       |
| **Free adult**                     | A child or enslaved person does not pay Zakat (the guardian/owner pays on their behalf in some cases) |
| **Owns the Nisab**                 | Possesses wealth above the minimum threshold                                                          |
| **One full lunar year has passed** | The wealth must have been above Nisab for one complete Hawl (lunar year)                              |
| **Zakatable asset**                | The wealth must be of a type on which Zakat is due                                                    |

---

## The Nisab The Minimum Threshold

The Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth above which Zakat becomes due. It is calculated based on the value of **gold** or **silver**:

| Standard         | Quantity            | Approximate Modern Value          |
| ---------------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Gold Nisab**   | 85 grams of gold    | Varies check current gold price   |
| **Silver Nisab** | 595 grams of silver | Varies check current silver price |

{% hint style="warning" %}
** Scholarly note:** Most contemporary scholars recommend using the **silver Nisab** as it is lower and therefore more inclusive meaning more people pay Zakat and more poor people benefit. Some scholars use gold. Consult your local scholar or Zakat organization for the current Nisab value in your currency.
{% endhint %}

If your total zakatable wealth (cash, gold, silver, stock, trade goods) equals or exceeds the Nisab and has been held for a full lunar year Zakat is due.

---

## How Much Is Zakat?

The standard Zakat rate is **2.5%** of the total zakatable wealth above the Nisab.

**Example:**

- Total savings + gold + stocks = $15,000
- Nisab = $4,500 (approximate, based on silver)
- Zakatable amount = $15,000
- Zakat due = $15,000 × 2.5% = **$375**

Different types of assets have different Zakat rates:

| Asset                                    | Zakat Rate                  |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Cash, gold, silver, trade goods          | 2.5%                        |
| Agricultural produce (irrigated)         | 5%                          |
| Agricultural produce (rain-fed)          | 10%                         |
| Livestock (specific scales)              | Varies by animal and number |
| Minerals / Rikaz (buried treasure found) | 20%                         |

## What Assets Is Zakat Due On?

| Zakatable Asset                   | Notes                                                     |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cash** (savings, bank accounts) | After one lunar year above Nisab                          |
| **Gold and silver**               | Jewelry worn regularly debated; stored gold/silver agreed |
| **Stocks and shares**             | On the market value                                       |
| **Business / trade goods**        | On the value of inventory                                 |
| **Rental income**                 | On savings accumulated from it                            |
| **Cryptocurrency**                | Treated as a trade asset by most contemporary scholars    |

**Not Zakatable:**

- Your home (that you live in)
- Your personal vehicle
- Furniture and personal belongings
- Tools used in your profession

## The Eight Recipients of Zakat

The Quran specifies exactly who may receive Zakat:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah At-Tawbah 9:60**

**Translation:** "Zakat expenditures are only for the poor and for the needy and for those employed to collect it and for bringing hearts together and for freeing captives and for those in debt and for the cause of Allah and for the stranded traveler an obligation imposed by Allah. And Allah is Knowing and Wise."
{% endhint %}

| #   | Arabic                     | English                 | Description                                                                 |
| --- | -------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | الْفُقَرَاء                | Al-Fuqara               | The poor those with little or no income                                     |
| 2   | الْمَسَاكِين               | Al-Masakin              | The needy those with some income but not enough                             |
| 3   | الْعَامِلِينَ عَلَيْهَا    | Al-Amileen              | Zakat collectors and administrators                                         |
| 4   | الْمُؤَلَّفَةِ قُلُوبُهُمْ | Al-Mu'allafah Qulubuhum | Those whose hearts are to be reconciled new Muslims or those drawn to Islam |
| 5   | فِي الرِّقَاب              | Fir-Riqab               | Freeing of slaves and captives                                              |
| 6   | الْغَارِمِين               | Al-Gharimeen            | Those in debt unable to repay                                               |
| 7   | فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ        | Fi Sabilillah           | In the cause of Allah including Islamic education, da'wah, and defense      |
| 8   | ابْنِ السَّبِيل            | Ibn as-Sabil            | The stranded traveler with no access to their own funds                     |

{% hint style="tip" %}
** Practical note:** In most modern contexts, the most relevant categories are (1) the poor, (2) the needy, (6) those in debt, and (7) Islamic causes. Many Muslims pay Zakat directly to established charities or organizations that distribute to eligible recipients this is valid and encouraged.
{% endhint %}

## Who Cannot Receive Zakat?

- **Non-Muslims** Zakat is specifically for Muslims (though Sadaqah is encouraged for all)
- **The giver's own immediate dependents** spouse, children, parents
- **The wealthy** those above the Nisab
- **Banu Hashim** the Prophet's family (by some scholarly positions)

## The Spiritual Wisdom of Zakat

**1. It breaks the love of wealth.** The Prophet said: _"The love of wealth and honor destroys a person's religion as surely as two hungry wolves released among a flock of sheep."_ Zakat is an annual act of detachment a reminder that wealth is a trust from Allah, not an identity.

**2. It builds community.** Zakat creates a financial safety net for the Muslim community institutionalizing care for the poor, the indebted, and the stranded.

**3. It purifies and grows.** The Quran promises:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Baqarah 2:261**

**Translation:** "The example of those who spend their wealth in the way of Allah is like a seed which grows seven spikes in each spike is a hundred grains. Allah multiplies for whom He wills, and Allah is all-Encompassing and Knowing."

_One grain of charity → 700 grains of reward in the Hereafter._
{% endhint %}

## Key Takeaways

- Zakat is the third pillar of Islam obligatory upon every qualifying Muslim adult.
- It is due at 2.5% of zakatable wealth held above the Nisab for one full lunar year.
- The Nisab is approximately 85g of gold or 595g of silver check the current value in your currency.
- The Quran specifies eight categories of recipients the poor, the needy, the indebted, and others.
- Zakat purifies the giver spiritually, redistributes wealth to those in need, and earns immense divine reward.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<details>

<summary>Do I pay Zakat on gold jewelry my wife wears daily?</summary>

This is a classic area of scholarly difference. The Hanafi school holds that Zakat is due on all gold and silver regardless of use. The Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali schools hold that regularly worn jewelry is exempt. Both positions have strong evidence. Choose the position of your madhab or err on the side of caution by paying.

</details>

<details>

<summary>What if I forgot to pay Zakat for previous years?</summary>

You must calculate what was due and pay it Zakat is a debt owed to Allah and to those who were entitled to it. Scholars recommend calculating year by year to the best of your ability and paying the full amount with the sincere intention of making good what was missed.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Is Zakat different from income tax?</summary>

Yes fundamentally. Zakat is an act of worship owed to Allah. Tax is a legal obligation owed to a government. Paying income tax does not fulfill Zakat. They are separate obligations. However, the way Zakat is managed in some countries (collected by Islamic authorities and distributed systematically) resembles a social welfare tax in structure which is intentional. Islam institutionalized welfare 1,400 years before the modern welfare state.

</details>
