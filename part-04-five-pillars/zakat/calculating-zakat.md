# Calculating Your Zakat A Step-by-Step Guide

**Difficulty:** Beginner &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~8 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 4 Five Pillars Zakat

---

## What This Guide Covers

> Zakat calculation intimidates many Muslims but it follows a clear, systematic process. This guide walks you through calculating your annual Zakat step by step, with worked examples and guidance on common assets.

---

## The Three Key Numbers You Need

Before calculating, you need to know three things:

| What                                 | Why                                                           |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Today's Nisab value**              | The minimum wealth threshold that makes Zakat obligatory      |
| **Your total zakatable assets**      | What you own that Zakat applies to                            |
| **Whether one full year has passed** | Zakat is due on wealth held above Nisab for a full lunar year |

---

## Step 1 Find Today's Nisab Value

The Nisab is the minimum threshold. It is based on the value of **85 grams of gold** or **595 grams of silver**.

**Find current values:**

- Search: _"gold price per gram today"_ → multiply by 85
- Search: _"silver price per gram today"_ → multiply by 595

{% hint style="info" %}
**Which Nisab to use gold or silver?**

The silver Nisab is typically lower, making more people obligated to pay and more poor people benefiting. Most contemporary scholars recommend the silver Nisab.

**Example (approximate check current prices):**

- If gold is $65/gram → Gold Nisab = 85 × $65 = **$5,525**
- If silver is $0.85/gram → Silver Nisab = 595 × $0.85 = **$506**

At these prices, many scholars would say: if your total zakatable wealth exceeds $506, Zakat is due.
{% endhint %}

---

## Step 2 List Your Zakatable Assets

Go through each category and write down your current value:

### Zakatable Assets

| Asset Category          | What to Include                                       | Your Value |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| **Cash at home**        | Physical currency                                     | $          |
| **Bank accounts**       | Current balance (checking + savings)                  | $          |
| **Investments**         | Stocks, mutual funds at current market value          | $          |
| **Gold and silver**     | Jewelry (some madhabs), stored gold/silver, gold ETFs | $          |
| **Business inventory**  | Stock/goods for sale at market value                  | $          |
| **Loans receivable**    | Money others owe you (that you expect to recover)     | $          |
| **Cryptocurrency**      | Market value at Zakat date                            | $          |
| **Rental income saved** | Savings from rental income above personal expenses    | $          |

### Non-Zakatable Assets

| Asset                             | Why Excluded                          |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Your primary home                 | Personal residence not surplus wealth |
| Your car (personal use)           | Personal necessity                    |
| Furniture, electronics            | Personal belongings                   |
| Pension/retirement funds (locked) | Not accessible most scholars exclude  |
| Business equipment                | Tools of trade, not inventory         |

---

## Step 3 Subtract Your Liabilities

Deduct debts that are **due within the year**:

| Liability                                | Deduct?                       |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Mortgage installment due this month/year | Yes the amount due this year  |
| Credit card balance                      | Yes                           |
| Personal loans due                       | Yes                           |
| Future installments not yet due          | No only what is currently due |

{% hint style="warning" %}
** Madhab difference on debt deduction:**

The majority position: deduct debts from total zakatable assets before calculating. If assets minus debts fall below Nisab no Zakat is due.

Some scholars hold that debts do not affect Zakat calculation. Follow your madhab's ruling.
{% endhint %}

**Net Zakatable Wealth = Total Zakatable Assets − Debts Due**

---

## Step 4 Confirm One Full Lunar Year Has Passed

Zakat is due when zakatable wealth has been **above the Nisab for one complete lunar year** (approximately 354 days).

**How to track your Zakat year:**

- Choose a date (many Muslims use Ramadan 1st easy to remember)
- Check if your wealth exceeded Nisab on that date last year
- If it did, and it still exceeds Nisab today Zakat is due
- If wealth dropped below Nisab during the year and came back up a new year begins from when it came back above Nisab

---

## Step 5 Calculate 2.5%

**Zakat Due = Net Zakatable Wealth × 2.5%**

### Worked Example

| Item                                     | Amount      |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Savings account                          | $12,000     |
| Investment account                       | $8,000      |
| Gold jewelry (Hanafi all gold zakatable) | $3,000      |
| Cash at home                             | $500        |
| **Total Zakatable Assets**               | **$23,500** |
| Minus: credit card balance due           | −$1,500     |
| **Net Zakatable Wealth**                 | **$22,000** |
| Nisab (silver basis, approximate)        | $506        |
| **Above Nisab?**                         | Yes         |
| **Zakat Due: $22,000 × 2.5%**            | **$550**    |

## Special Cases

### Gold Jewelry (Women's Worn Jewelry)

| Madhab                       | Ruling                                                   |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Hanafi**                   | Zakat due on all gold and silver, including worn jewelry |
| **Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali** | No Zakat on jewelry worn regularly for personal use      |

**Recommended approach:** If in doubt, pay Zakat on jewelry. It is better to give more than to under-give.

### Stocks and Shares

**If you own shares in a company:** Pay Zakat on:

- The _Zakatable portion_ of the company's assets (cash, receivables, inventory) proportional to your shareholding detailed calculation requires the company's _Zakat-eligible asset_ report (many Islamic funds provide this)

**Simplified approach (widely accepted):** Pay 2.5% on the current market value of your shares if you hold them as an investment (not for active trading). If held for active trading treat as business inventory.

### Pension and Retirement Funds

Most scholars: if the funds are locked and inaccessible, they are not zakatable until you receive them. When received pay Zakat on one full year's worth retroactively.

## Where to Pay Zakat

**Options:**

| Option                              | Considerations                                                                                |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Directly to eligible recipients** | Most reward direct relationship; must verify eligibility                                      |
| **Local mosque Zakat fund**         | Convenient; distributes to local community                                                    |
| **Established Zakat charities**     | National Zakat Foundation (UK/USA), Islamic Relief, Human Appeal, Zakat Foundation of America |
| **Zakat to family in need**         | Permitted but not to immediate dependents (spouse, children, parents)                         |

---

## Key Takeaways

- Zakat = 2.5% of net zakatable wealth above the Nisab, after one lunar year.
- Nisab today: check current silver price × 595 grams (most scholars recommend silver basis).
- Zakatable: cash, savings, investments, gold, business inventory, crypto.
- Non-zakatable: home, car, personal belongings, locked pension funds.
- Subtract debts due this year before calculating.
- Choose a consistent annual Zakat date Ramadan 1st is easy to remember.
