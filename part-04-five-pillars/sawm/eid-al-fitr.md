## What Is Eid al-Fitr?

Eid al-Fitr (_عِيدُ الفِطْر_) the Festival of Breaking the Fast falls on the 1st of Shawwal, the month following Ramadan. It is one of the two great Islamic celebrations, marking the completion of a month of fasting with a day of joy, gratitude, and community.

The Prophet said: _"Every people has a celebration, and this is our celebration."_ (Sahih al-Bukhari)

---

## Zakat al-Fitr The Obligatory Charity of Eid

**Before the Eid prayer**, every Muslim who has food in excess of their needs must give _Zakat al-Fitr_:

- Amount: Approximately **2.5kg of staple food** (or its cash equivalent) per person in the household
- Recipients: The poor so they too can celebrate Eid
- Timing: Must be given before the Eid prayer; given after the prayer is only regular Sadaqah

The Prophet said: _"The Prophet obligated Zakat al-Fitr as a purification for the fasting person from idle and obscene speech, and as food for the poor."_ (Abu Dawud Sahih)

## The Eid Prayer

- **Two Rak'ahs** with extra Takbirat (7 in first Rak'ah, 5 in second before Ruku')
- Followed by **two Khutbahs** (sermons)
- Prayed in congregation in open ground (_Musalla_) when possible

---

## The Spirit of Eid

Eid is not merely a reward for fasting it is the celebration of _Taqwa_ achieved, of a community that submitted together for a month. It is a day to:

- Visit family and strengthen ties
- Give gifts, especially to children
- Feed the poor and include them in the celebration
- Express gratitude to Allah with joy not guilt about the month ending
