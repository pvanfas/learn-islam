# Non-Muslim Historians and Thinkers on Muhammad

**Difficulty:** Intermediate &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~10 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 6 Seerah

---

## In This Chapter

> The Prophet Muhammad is among the most written-about human beings in history by Muslims and non-Muslims alike. This chapter presents the assessments of prominent non-Muslim historians, philosophers, writers, and thinkers who studied his life many of whom arrived at conclusions that surprised their own readers.

---

## Why This Matters

For Muslim readers: hearing how the Prophet is assessed by those who had no theological reason to honor him yet arrived at deep admiration is a powerful confirmation.

For non-Muslim readers: these voices from Western scholars, historians, and philosophers represent the honest intellectual engagement with the Seerah that transcends religious boundaries.

---

## The Historians and Their Words

---

### Michael Hart _The 100: A Ranking of the Most Influential Persons in History_ (1978)

Michael Hart an American astrophysicist and historian ranked the 100 most influential persons in all of human history. His first choice was controversial:

**Muhammad was ranked #1 above Jesus, Newton, and all other figures in human history.**

Hart's reasoning was explicit:

> _"My choice of Muhammad to lead the list of the world's most influential persons may surprise some readers and may be questioned by others, but he was the only man in history who was supremely successful on both the religious and secular levels."_

He noted that unlike other religious founders, Muhammad was not only a prophet but a political leader, military commander, lawgiver, and social reformer and that he achieved all of this within a single lifetime from an unpromising beginning.

---

### Thomas Carlyle _On Heroes, Hero-Worship, and the Heroic in History_ (1840)

Scottish historian Thomas Carlyle devoted a chapter to Muhammad challenging the caricatures of his era with force:

> _"How one man single-handedly, could weld warring tribes and wandering Bedouins into a most powerful and civilized nation in less than two decades."_

> _"A silent great soul... he was to kindle the world."_

Carlyle attacked the then-fashionable claim that the Prophet was an impostor or a fraud arguing that no man who lied to gain power could have produced what he produced:

> _"The lies which well-meaning zeal has heaped around this man are disgraceful to ourselves only."_

---

### George Bernard Shaw _The Genuine Islam_ (1936)

The Irish playwright and Nobel laureate wrote:

> _"He must be called the Saviour of Humanity. I believe that if a man like him were to assume the dictatorship of the modern world, he would succeed in solving its problems in a way that would bring it the much needed peace and happiness."_

> _"I have always held the religion of Muhammad in high estimation because of its wonderful vitality. It is the only religion which appears to me to possess that assimilating capacity to the changing phase of existence which can make itself appeal to every age."_

---

### Washington Irving _Life of Mahomet_ (1850)

The American author of _The Legend of Sleepy Hollow_ wrote a detailed biography:

> _"He was sober and abstemious in his diet and a rigorous observer of fasts. He indulged in no magnificence of apparel, the ostentation of a petty mind; neither was his simplicity in dress affected but was the result of a real disregard for distinction from so trivial a source."_

> _"In his private dealings he was just. He treated friends and strangers, the rich and poor, the powerful and the weak, with equity, and was beloved by the common people for the affability with which he received them, and listened to their complaints."_

---

### Alphonse de Lamartine _Histoire de la Turquie_ (1854)

The French historian and statesman wrote what remains one of the most celebrated non-Muslim tributes:

> _"If greatness of purpose, smallness of means, and astounding results are the three criteria of a human genius, who could dare compare any great man in history with Muhammad? The most famous men created arms, laws, and empires only. They founded, if anything at all, no more than material powers which often crumbled away before their eyes. This man moved not only armies, legislations, empires, peoples, and dynasties, but millions of men in one-third of the then inhabited world; and more than that, he moved the altars, the gods, the religions, the ideas, the beliefs and the souls."_

---

### Annie Besant _The Life and Teachings of Mohammed_ (1932)

The British socialist and theosophist:

> _"It is impossible for anyone who studies the life and character of the great Prophet of Arabia, who knows how he taught and how he lived, to feel anything but reverence for that mighty Prophet, one of the great messengers of the Supreme. And although in what I put to you I shall say many things which may be familiar to many, yet I myself feel, whenever I reread them, a new way of admiration, a new sense of reverence for that mighty Arabian teacher."_

---

### William Muir _The Life of Mahomet_ (1858)

Muir was one of the most critical Western biographers of the Prophet yet even he acknowledged:

> _"The sword of Muhammad and the Quran are the most stubborn enemies of civilization, liberty, and truth which the world has yet known... But the personal courage and intrepidity of Muhammad himself in prosecuting the task he had undertaken cannot be denied."_

And on the Quran: _"There is probably in the world no other book which has remained twelve centuries with so pure a text."_

Even critics of the mission acknowledged the man's character and the text's integrity.

---

### Mahatma Gandhi _Young India_ (1924)

> _"I wanted to know the best of the life of one who holds today an undisputed sway over the hearts of millions of mankind... I became more than ever convinced that it was not the sword that won a place for Islam in those days in the scheme of life. It was the rigid simplicity, the utter self-effacement of the Prophet, the scrupulous regard for pledges, his intense devotion to his friends and followers, his intrepidity, his fearlessness, his absolute trust in God and in his own mission."_

> _"These and not the sword carried everything before them and surmounted every obstacle."_

Gandhi who led non-violent resistance to the British Empire specifically cited the Prophet's non-coercive methods as the key to Islam's spread.

---

### Jules Masserman _Who Were History's Great Leaders?_ (LIFE Magazine, 1974)

The American psychoanalyst defined great leadership through three criteria: providing for followers' well-being, providing a social organization, and providing a belief system. He concluded:

> _"Perhaps the greatest leader of all times was Mohammed, who combined all three functions. To a lesser degree, Moses did the same."_

---

### Reverend Bosworth Smith _Mohammed and Mohammedanism_ (1874)

> _"He was Caesar and Pope in one; but he was Pope without Pope's pretensions, Caesar without the legions of Caesar: without a standing army, without a bodyguard, without a palace, without a fixed revenue; if ever any man had the right to say that he ruled by the right divine, it was Mohammed, for he had all the power without its instruments and without its supports."_

---

## What These Voices Have in Common

Across different eras, nationalities, religions, and purposes these thinkers converge on several observations:

1. **The contrast between means and results** an orphan with no army, no wealth, no political position transformed a world
2. **The integrity of the man** even hostile critics acknowledged his personal honesty, simplicity, and consistency
3. **The universality of the message** Islam's appeal across cultures, races, and centuries without diminishing
4. **The preservation of the text** the Quran's textual integrity acknowledged even by critics

These are not Muslim hagiographies they are the honest assessments of people who studied the evidence and wrote what they found.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Michael Hart (American historian) ranked Muhammad first in the 100 most influential persons in human history ahead of all religious, scientific, and political figures.
- Carlyle, Lamartine, Shaw, Gandhi, and others acknowledged the transformation he achieved with minimal means as historically unparalleled.
- Even critical biographers like William Muir acknowledged the man's character and the Quran's textual integrity.
- The common thread: integrity, simplicity, universality, and the extraordinary ratio of achievement to material means.
- Muslims' love for the Prophet is confirmed, from the outside, by those who had no theological stake in that love.
