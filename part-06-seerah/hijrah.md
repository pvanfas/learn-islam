# The Hijrah — Migration to Madinah

**Difficulty:** 🟢 Beginner &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~8 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 6 — Seerah

---

## 📌 In This Chapter

> The Hijrah — the migration from Makkah to Madinah in 622 CE — is the most significant political and spiritual event in early Islamic history. It marks the birth of the Muslim community as a state and the beginning of the Islamic calendar. This chapter covers why it was necessary, how it was executed, and why Muslims still count time from this moment.

---

## 🌙 Why the Hijrah Was Necessary

By 622 CE, the mission in Makkah had reached an impasse:

- The Prophet ﷺ had lost Abu Talib's political protection
- The torture of Muslims was escalating toward assassination of the Prophet ﷺ himself
- Several attempts on his life had already been made
- No significant conversion among Makkah's power elite was forthcoming

But a new door was opening. During the Hajj season of 620 and 621 CE, the Prophet ﷺ had met groups from the city of **Yathrib** (later renamed Al-Madinah) — they were already familiar with the concept of prophethood through their Jewish neighbors, and many were deeply impressed by the Prophet ﷺ.

At the **Second Pledge of Aqabah** (622 CE), 73 men and 2 women from Yathrib formally pledged allegiance to the Prophet ﷺ — promising to protect him as they would protect their own family, and inviting him and the Muslim community to their city. The stage was set.

---

## 📋 The Plan of the Quraysh — And Allah's Response

When the Quraysh heard of the Madinan pledges, they convened an emergency council. Their plan: representatives from every major clan would simultaneously strike the Prophet ﷺ — so that the responsibility and blood guilt would be spread across all clans, making retaliation by Banu Hashim impossible.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Anfal 8:30**

**Translation:** "And remember when the disbelievers plotted against you — to confine you, kill you, or expel you. They plotted and Allah plotted — and Allah is the best of plotters."
{% endhint %}

Jibreel (AS) came to the Prophet ﷺ that night and warned him not to sleep in his bed. The Prophet ﷺ asked Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA) to sleep in his bed wearing his green cloak — so that the watching assassins would think he was still there — and left quietly with Abu Bakr (RA).

When the assassins rushed in at dawn, they found Ali — unharmed, calm, and utterly unafraid.

---

## 🏜️ The Journey — Three Days in the Cave of Thawr

The Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr (RA) moved south (opposite the direction of Madinah — to confuse pursuers) and hid in **Cave Thawr** on a mountain outside Makkah for three days while the Quraysh sent search parties with rewards of 100 camels for the Prophet ﷺ dead or alive.

At one moment, a search party came directly to the cave entrance. Abu Bakr (RA), terrified for the Prophet ﷺ, whispered: *"O Messenger of Allah — if any of them looks down at their feet they will see us!"*

The Prophet ﷺ replied with complete tranquility:

{% hint style="info" %}
*"What do you think of two — when Allah is their third?"*

**Surah At-Tawbah 9:40**

**Translation:** "...when they were in the cave and he said to his companion: 'Do not grieve — indeed Allah is with us.' And Allah sent down His tranquility (*Sakinah*) upon him and supported him with soldiers you did not see."
{% endhint %}

The search party stood at the cave entrance — but did not enter. A spider had spun a web across the opening and a dove had nested and laid eggs at the entrance. It appeared that no one could have entered recently. The Quraysh moved on.

---

## 🤝 Arrival in Madinah — The Ansar's Welcome

After three days, the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr (RA) mounted camels and set out for Madinah via an unfamiliar coastal route — guided by Abdullah ibn Urayqit, a non-Muslim guide they had hired for his knowledge of the terrain.

When they arrived at the outskirts of Madinah, the city came out to receive them. The women and children of the Ansar sang from the rooftops:

*"Tala'al badru 'alayna..."*
*"The full moon has risen over us — from the valley of Wada'..."*

Every family wanted the honor of hosting the Prophet ﷺ. He resolved the competition with wisdom: *"Leave the camel — she is commanded. She will stop where Allah wishes."* The camel knelt at a date-drying ground owned by two orphans — and that became the site of **Masjid an-Nabawi**, the Prophet's Mosque.

## 📅 Why the Islamic Calendar Begins with the Hijrah

When the second Caliph Umar (RA) formalized the Islamic calendar (establishing a dating system for the growing Muslim administration), he faced a choice: which event should mark Year 1?

The birth of the Prophet ﷺ? His first revelation? The Night Journey?

Umar consulted the senior Companions. **Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA)** recommended the Hijrah — and his reasoning was accepted unanimously:

The Hijrah was the moment the Muslim community became a **reality** — not just a message, but a functioning society with its own governance, law, and institutions. It was the beginning of Islam as a civilization. Everything before was preparation; everything after was building.

This is why the Islamic calendar is called **AH (After Hijrah)** — *Anno Hegirae* in Latin, or *Hijri* in Arabic.

## 🌟 The Spiritual Meaning of the Hijrah

The Hijrah is not merely a historical event — it is a permanent principle in Islamic spirituality:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sahih al-Bukhari**

The Prophet ﷺ said: *"The muhajir (migrant) is one who migrates from what Allah has forbidden."*
{% endhint %}

Every Muslim is called to a form of Hijrah — not necessarily a geographic one, but an internal one: migrating from what Allah has forbidden, from the old self, from the habits and attachments that distance the heart from Allah.

The Quraysh-controlled Makkah is a symbol of any environment that suppresses faith, virtue, and truth. The Madinah of the Prophet ﷺ is a symbol of any community built on divine values.

---

## ✅ Key Takeaways

- The Hijrah was made necessary by the escalation of persecution and the impossibility of growth in Makkah.
- The Quraysh planned a simultaneous assassination — Allah revealed the plan and the Prophet ﷺ escaped with Abu Bakr (RA).
- Three days in Cave Thawr — the Prophet's ﷺ calm assurance of divine protection against the Quraysh's search.
- The Ansar's extraordinary welcome marked a new beginning for the Muslim community.
- The Islamic calendar (Hijri) begins with the Hijrah — marking the birth of Islam as a civilization.
- The Hijrah is a permanent spiritual principle: every Muslim migrates internally from what Allah has forbidden.
