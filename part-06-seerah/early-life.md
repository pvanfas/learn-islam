# Early Life of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

**Difficulty:** Beginner &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~9 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 6 — Seerah

---

## In This Chapter

> Before prophethood, Muhammad ﷺ lived a life of remarkable character — an orphan who became the most trusted man in Makkah. This chapter covers his birth, the successive losses that shaped him, his years as a young man, and the qualities that made even his enemies call him _Al-Amin_ — the Trustworthy.

## The Birth — 570 CE, the Year of the Elephant

Muhammad ibn Abdillah was born in **Makkah** in approximately **570 CE** — a year the Arabs called _Amul Fil_ — the Year of the Elephant. That same year, the Yemeni general Abraha had marched on Makkah with a war elephant at the head of his army, intending to destroy the Ka'bah. He was turned back by a miraculous event — birds (_Ababil_) dropping stones of baked clay — described in Surah Al-Fil.

His father, **Abdullah ibn Abd al-Muttalib**, had died before his birth — while on a trading journey to Madinah. Muhammad ﷺ was born an orphan.

His mother, **Aminah bint Wahb**, reported that at the moment of his birth she saw a light emerge from her that illuminated the palaces of Syria.

---

## Life with Halimah in the Desert

According to Arab custom, wealthy Makkan families sent their newborns to the desert to be raised by Bedouin wet nurses — for the health benefits of desert air and to ensure the child learned pure, classical Arabic free from city dialects.

The young Muhammad ﷺ was entrusted to **Halimah al-Sa'diyyah** of the Banu Sa'd tribe. She later reported that from the moment he arrived in their care, their camels began producing more milk, their animals became healthier, and their household prospered in ways they had never experienced before — they attributed it all to the blessed presence of the child.

### The Incident of the Opening of the Chest

While Muhammad ﷺ was still a young child in Halimah's care, the Companions narrate that two men in white (identified in other narrations as angels) came to him, laid him down, opened his chest, removed his heart, cleansed it, and returned it — all while he was conscious. Halimah's family, alarmed, returned him to his mother in Makkah.

This event — the _Shaqq al-Sadr_ (opening of the chest) — is narrated in Sahih Muslim and referenced in the Quran:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Ash-Sharh 94:1–3**

_أَلَمْ نَشْرَحْ لَكَ صَدْرَكَ_

**Translation:** "Did We not expand your breast for you? And removed from you your burden that had weighed upon your back?"
{% endhint %}

It happened again before the Isra and Mi'raj (Night Journey) — as recorded in Sahih Muslim.

## A Childhood of Loss

The life of the Prophet ﷺ before prophethood was marked by successive losses that would have broken lesser souls:

| Age          | Loss                                                                 |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Before birth | His father Abdullah died                                             |
| ~6 years old | His mother Aminah died — returning from a visit to Madinah           |
| ~8 years old | His grandfather Abd al-Muttalib died — his guardian after his mother |
| Adult life   | His uncle Abu Talib — his greatest protector — died                  |
| Adult life   | His first wife Khadijah (RA) died in the same year                   |

The year Abu Talib and Khadijah both died is known in the Seerah as **Amul Huzn** — the Year of Grief.

These losses were not incidental. They shaped the Prophet ﷺ into a man of extraordinary empathy for orphans, widows, the poor, and the bereaved. The Quran would later remind him — and through him, all of us:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Ad-Duha 93:6–8**

**Translation:** "Did He not find you an orphan and give you shelter? And He found you lost and guided you. And He found you poor and made you self-sufficient."
{% endhint %}

## Youth — Trade, Travel, and Character

After his grandfather's death, Muhammad ﷺ was taken in by his uncle **Abu Talib**, the chief of the Banu Hashim clan of Quraysh — a man of honor and courage who would protect his nephew for decades despite never accepting Islam.

Muhammad ﷺ traveled with his uncle on trade caravans to Syria as a teenager. On one journey, a Christian monk named **Bahira** in Busra (Syria) recognized signs of prophethood in the young Muhammad ﷺ — the shade of a cloud following him, the bowing of trees — and urged Abu Talib to protect him and return him to Makkah.

### Hilf al-Fudul — Standing Against Injustice

As a young man, Muhammad ﷺ participated in a noble pact called **Hilf al-Fudul** — formed by several Makkan tribes to protect the oppressed and ensure justice in trade disputes. It was established after a Yemeni trader was cheated by a Makkan nobleman and had no recourse.

Years after prophethood, the Prophet ﷺ said: _"I witnessed in the house of Abdullah ibn Jud'an a pact so noble that I would not exchange my part in it for a herd of red camels. If I were invited to it in Islam, I would respond."_

This shows that the Prophet ﷺ honored pre-Islamic acts of justice — Islam came to perfect good, not to reject every pre-Islamic value.

## Marriage to Khadijah (RA)

**Khadijah bint Khuwaylid** was one of the wealthiest and most respected women in Makkah. She was a successful merchant who employed men to trade on her behalf. She heard of Muhammad's ﷺ exceptional trustworthiness and hired him to lead a trade caravan to Syria.

His conduct on the journey so impressed her — and the profits he returned with were so exceptional — that she sent a proposal of marriage to him through an intermediary.

He was 25 years old. She was approximately 40.

They married — and their marriage was by every account one of extraordinary love, partnership, and mutual respect.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Khadijah (RA)** would become the first person to accept Islam. When the first revelation came and the Prophet ﷺ returned home trembling, it was her arms he ran to. It was her voice that calmed him: _"By Allah, Allah will never humiliate you. You maintain family ties, you speak the truth, you bear the burdens of others, you help the destitute, you serve your guests, and you support those who stand for truth."_ (Sahih al-Bukhari)

She described his character — before a single word of the Quran had been revealed.
{% endhint %}

They had six children together: two sons (Qasim and Abdullah, both of whom died in infancy) and four daughters (Zaynab, Ruqayyah, Umm Kulthum, and Fatimah).

Muhammad ﷺ never took another wife during Khadijah's lifetime — a significant fact given the polygamous norms of his society.

## Rebuilding the Ka'bah — The Wisdom of the Black Stone

When Muhammad ﷺ was about 35 years old, the Ka'bah was damaged by floods and needed to be rebuilt. The Quraysh undertook the task — but when the time came to return the **Black Stone** (_Hajar al-Aswad_) to its place in the corner of the Ka'bah, a bitter dispute broke out between the tribes. Each tribe wanted the honor of placing it.

They agreed that the next person to enter the sanctuary would be their judge. That person was Muhammad ﷺ.

He asked for a large cloth, placed the Black Stone in its center, and asked a representative of each tribe to hold one corner and lift it together. He then placed the Stone himself — sharing the honor equally among all the tribes.

This moment — before prophethood — showed a man of natural wisdom, fairness, and the ability to solve complex social problems with elegant simplicity.

## The Years of Seclusion — Before the Call

As he approached 40, Muhammad ﷺ began spending extended periods in a cave on the mountain of Hira (_Jabal an-Nur_ — the Mountain of Light) outside Makkah — alone, in reflection and worship. He would take provisions and spend days and nights there in contemplation.

He felt, with increasing intensity, a profound unease with the idol worship around him, with the social injustice of Makkah, with the spiritual emptiness of his world. He was searching — though he did not yet know what he was searching for.

He would not wait long.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The Prophet ﷺ was born in 570 CE in Makkah — the Year of the Elephant — and was an orphan from birth.
- He was raised by his wet nurse Halimah in the desert, then by his grandfather, then his uncle Abu Talib.
- A childhood of loss gave him extraordinary empathy — for orphans, the bereaved, and the poor.
- He earned the title _Al-Amin_ (the Trustworthy) through decades of impeccable character before prophethood.
- His marriage to Khadijah (RA) — a wealthy, accomplished businesswoman 15 years his senior — was by proposal and was a partnership of deep love and mutual respect.
- The Black Stone incident showed his natural wisdom and ability to resolve conflicts equitably.
- By age 40 he was spending long periods in spiritual seclusion — on the eve of receiving the greatest responsibility ever given to a human being.
