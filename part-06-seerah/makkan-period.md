## In This Chapter

> For thirteen years in Makkah, the early Muslims were a tiny, persecuted minority tortured, boycotted, and driven to the edge of extinction. This chapter covers the nature of the Quraysh's opposition, how the early Muslims endured, the migration to Abyssinia, the Year of Sorrow, and the miraculous Night Journey that prepared the Prophet for the next chapter.

## Why the Quraysh Opposed Islam

The Quraysh's opposition to the Prophet was not primarily theological it was economic and political. The Ka'bah's 360 idols were their livelihood. Pilgrims from across Arabia came to Makkah to worship spending money, trading, and bringing prestige to the Quraysh. Islam's declaration of _La ilaha illallah_ threatened to destroy this entire system.

Beyond economics, Islam's social message was radical declaring all people equal before Allah regardless of tribe, wealth, or lineage. This challenged the entire social hierarchy from which the Quraysh derived their authority.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Qasas 28:57**

The Quraysh said: _"If we follow the guidance with you, we will be swept away from our land."_

_They knew what accepting Islam meant: the end of their political and economic dominance._
{% endhint %}

## The Torture of the Early Muslims

When direct argument failed to stop the Prophet , the Quraysh turned to persecution targeting primarily those who had no tribal protection: freed slaves, the poor, and women.

### Bilal ibn Rabah (RA) The First Muezzin

Bilal was an enslaved man owned by Umayyah ibn Khalaf. When he accepted Islam, his master dragged him into the blazing Makkan midday heat, made him lie on burning sand, and placed a massive boulder on his chest ordering him to renounce Islam. Bilal's response was only: _"Ahad! Ahad!"_ One! One! affirming the Oneness of God.

Abu Bakr (RA) purchased Bilal and freed him. When the Prophet later asked who would call the Adhan, Bilal was chosen for his powerful voice the same voice that had declared Ahad in the sand.

### The Family of Yasir The First Martyrs

Yasir, his wife Sumayyah, and their son Ammar were among the early converts. Yasir and Sumayyah were tortured to death by Abu Jahl Sumayyah was the **first martyr in Islam**, killed by a spear thrust. Ammar (RA) was forced to say words of disbelief under torture and was heartbroken. The verse was revealed:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah An-Nahl 16:106**

**Translation:** "Whoever disbelieves in Allah after their belief except for one who is compelled while their heart is secure in faith..."

_This verse established that forced speech under torture, while the heart remains firm, is not apostasy. The soul's conviction is what matters._
{% endhint %}

### Khabbab ibn al-Aratt (RA)

He was a blacksmith who accepted Islam early. His owner placed burning coals on his bare back until they cooled from his flesh for no reason other than his faith.

The Prophet once found Khabbab and asked: _"Should we not pray to Allah to grant you victory?"_ Khabbab said the Prophet's question made him realize his own impatience the Prophet then told him of those who had come before, who were tortured with iron combs tearing flesh from bone yet did not abandon their faith.

## The Migration to Abyssinia 615 CE

Unable to protect his Companions from torture, the Prophet gave permission for the first Hijrah to **Abyssinia (Ethiopia)**, ruled by the Christian king Ashama ibn Abjar, known as **the Negus**.

_"Go to the land of Abyssinia, for there is a king there who wrongs no one. It is a land of truth until Allah opens a way out for you."_

Around 80100 Companions made this journey, including Uthman ibn Affan (RA) and his wife Ruqayyah (RA), the Prophet's daughter.

The Quraysh sent ambassadors to the Negus with gifts demanding the Muslims be returned. The Negus called the Muslims to his court and asked them to explain their beliefs. Jafar ibn Abi Talib (RA) the Prophet's cousin stood and gave an extraordinary presentation:

_"O King, we were a people of ignorance worshipping idols, eating carrion, committing shameful deeds, betraying our neighbors, and the strong among us consumed the weak. Then Allah sent us a messenger from among us we know his lineage, his truthfulness, his trustworthiness, his purity. He called us to worship Allah alone and to abandon what we and our ancestors worshipped of stones and idols. He commanded us to be truthful, to return trusts, to maintain family ties, to be good neighbors, and to refrain from the forbidden. So we believed in him and followed him."_

The Negus then asked about Jesus and Jafar recited from Surah Maryam (Chapter 19). The Negus wept until his beard was wet, and declared: _"By God, the difference between what you say and what we say is no greater than this"_ indicating a very small gap. He refused to hand the Muslims over.

The migration to Abyssinia taught the Ummah a lesson: Islam could be lived in a non-Muslim land, under a just non-Muslim ruler, without compromise of faith.

## The Conversion of Hamza and Umar (RA)

In approximately 615616 CE, two men of extraordinary strength accepted Islam transforming the community's position:

### Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib (RA)

The Prophet's uncle one of the most physically formidable men in Arabia accepted Islam after hearing that Abu Jahl had struck the Prophet . He went to Abu Jahl and struck him publicly, declaring his Islam. From that day, the early Muslims had a protector of legendary strength. He would later be called _Asadullah_ the Lion of Allah and die as a martyr at Uhud.

### Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA)

Umar had been one of Islam's fiercest opponents so fierce that the Prophet had made dua: _"O Allah, strengthen Islam with Umar ibn al-Khattab or Amr ibn Hisham (Abu Jahl)."_ (Tirmidhi)

The famous story of his conversion: he set out to kill the Prophet . He was met on the way and told his own sister had accepted Islam. He went to her house, heard Quran being recited, beat his sister's husband, then his sister then was struck by her words of courage: _"O Umar do what you will we will not abandon our faith."_ He saw blood on her face from his own hand, felt shame, asked to read what they had been reading and encountered the opening of Surah Ta-Ha. He went directly to the Prophet and accepted Islam.

The Prophet said: _"O Allah, by Umar's Islam, make Islam strong."_ The next day, the Muslims prayed publicly around the Ka'bah for the first time.

## The Boycott Three Years of Siege

The Quraysh imposed a total economic and social boycott on the **Banu Hashim clan** (the Prophet's family) whether they were Muslim or not. For approximately **three years** (617619 CE), the entire clan was confined to a mountain pass (_Shi'b Abi Talib_) and cut off from:

- Trade
- Marriage alliances
- Food supplies

They survived on tree leaves, leather scraps, and charity smuggled in at night by sympathizers. The Prophet's wife Khadijah (RA) spent her entire fortune supporting the community during this period.

The boycott eventually collapsed when even non-Muslim Makkans found it morally unconscionable a delegation went to the Quraysh leadership demanding its end.

---

## The Year of Grief 619 CE

Two months after the boycott ended, in the space of weeks, the Prophet lost the two people who had been his greatest support:

**Khadijah (RA)** his wife of 25 years, his first believer, the woman who had calmed him after the first revelation, who had spent her entire wealth for Islam, who had given him children died after the strain of the boycott years.

**Abu Talib** his uncle and protector the man whose political standing in Quraysh had kept the Prophet from being killed died. Without Abu Talib's protection, the Prophet was now politically vulnerable in Makkah.

The Prophet named this _Amul Huzn_ the Year of Grief. He attempted to find a new base in **Ta'if** but was driven out, stoned, and left bleeding in the orchards outside the city. The Angel Jibreel came with the Angel of the Mountains, offering to crush Ta'if between its two mountains. The Prophet refused: _"Perhaps Allah will produce from their descendants people who will worship Allah alone."_

## The Night Journey and Ascension (_Al-Isra wal-Mi'raj_)

In the darkest period of the Makkan mission after the Year of Grief, with enemies closing in Allah gave His Prophet a gift unlike any given to any human being:

**Al-Isra** the Night Journey: from Masjid al-Haram in Makkah to Masjid al-Aqsa in Jerusalem in a single night, riding the Buraq (a miraculous creature). At Al-Aqsa, the Prophet led all the previous prophets in prayer Ibrahim, Musa, Isa, and all the others as their Imam.

**Al-Mi'raj** the Ascension: from Al-Aqsa through the seven heavens, meeting the prophets at each level, until reaching the Lote Tree of the Uttermost End (_Sidrat al-Muntaha_) the boundary of creation that no created being had passed. Then directly before Allah in a closeness that defies description.

It was during Al-Mi'raj that the **five daily prayers** were given originally fifty, reduced to five through the intercession of Musa (AS), who knew from experience how much his people could bear. The reward remained as fifty.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Isra 17:1**

_سُبْحَانَ الَّذِي أَسْرَىٰ بِعَبْدِهِ لَيْلًا مِّنَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ إِلَى الْمَسْجِدِ الْأَقْصَى_

**Translation:** "Exalted is He who took His servant by night from Al-Masjid al-Haram to Al-Masjid al-Aqsa, whose surroundings We have blessed to show him of Our signs. Indeed, He is the Hearing, the Seeing."
{% endhint %}

When the Prophet reported this the next morning, many in Makkah mocked and some who had recently accepted Islam apostatized it seemed impossible. But Abu Bakr (RA), when told, responded immediately: _"If he said it then it is true."_ And from that day he earned the title _As-Siddiq_ the one who affirms truth without hesitation.

## Key Takeaways

- The Quraysh's opposition was primarily economic and political Islam threatened their control over the Ka'bah pilgrimage economy.
- The early Muslims endured systematic torture Bilal, the family of Yasir, Khabbab without abandoning their faith.
- The first Hijrah to Abyssinia showed Islam could be lived with dignity under a just non-Muslim ruler.
- The conversions of Hamza and Umar (RA) strengthened the Muslim community significantly.
- The three-year boycott and the Year of Grief (loss of Khadijah and Abu Talib) were the lowest point of the mission.
- The Night Journey (Al-Isra wal-Mi'raj) was Allah's gift at the darkest moment and the occasion of the five daily prayers.
