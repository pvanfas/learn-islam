# The Character of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

**Difficulty:** Beginner &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~11 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 6 — Seerah

---

## In This Chapter

> The Quran describes the Prophet ﷺ as being "upon a great character." This chapter explores what that character looked like in practice — his relationships with his wives and children, his humor and gentleness, his treatment of enemies, his simplicity, his mercy — and why Muslims across 1,400 years have loved him with a depth that remains unique in human religious history.

---

## Opening Verse

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Qalam 68:4**

_وَإِنَّكَ لَعَلَىٰ خُلُقٍ عَظِيمٍ_

**Translation:** "And indeed, you are of a great moral character."

_This is Allah's own assessment of His Prophet ﷺ. When Aisha (RA) was asked to describe the character of the Prophet ﷺ, she said: "His character was the Quran." He lived the Quran. He was the Quran in human form._
{% endhint %}

## As a Husband

The Prophet ﷺ was an extraordinarily devoted and loving husband. In a culture that treated women as property, he declared:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sunan Ibn Majah (Sahih)**

_"The best of you are those who are best to their wives — and I am the best of you to my wives."_
{% endhint %}

**With Khadijah (RA):** Even years after her death, his love for her was so deep that Aisha (RA) — his later wife — said she was never jealous of any living woman as she was jealous of Khadijah, because of how often the Prophet ﷺ spoke of her with love and longing. He would send gifts to her old friends. He wept when he heard her sister's voice, because it sounded like hers.

**With Aisha (RA):** He raced with her on foot in the desert. He drank from the same spot on the cup that she had drunk from. He consulted her on matters. He described her as the most beloved person to him. When he was ill in his final days, he asked permission from his other wives to be nursed in Aisha's room — and he died with his head on her chest.

**Practical partnership:** He helped with housework — sewing his own sandals, mending his own clothes, assisting in the kitchen. He never once struck a woman. He never belittled or humiliated. He apologized when he made a mistake.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sahih Muslim**

Aisha (RA) was asked: _"What did the Prophet ﷺ do at home?"_
She said: _"He was at the service of his family. When the time of prayer came, he would get up and go to pray."_
{% endhint %}

## As a Father

The Prophet ﷺ deeply loved his children and grandchildren, in ways that consistently amazed those around him.

**With Fatimah (RA) — his youngest daughter:**
When Fatimah entered a room, the Prophet ﷺ would stand up for her, kiss her between her eyes, take her hand, and seat her in his own place. She did the same for him. Their love was visible to everyone.

He said: _"Fatimah is a part of me. Whoever angers her, angers me."_ (Sahih al-Bukhari)

**With al-Hasan and al-Husayn (RA) — his grandsons:**
He would carry them on his back during prayer, prolonging his prostrations so as not to disturb them. He would allow them to climb on him while he was leading the people. He kissed them publicly in an era when Arab men rarely showed physical affection to children.

When a man saw the Prophet ﷺ kissing his grandson and said: _"I have ten children and I have never kissed any of them!"_ — the Prophet ﷺ replied: _"What can I do for you if Allah has removed mercy from your heart?"_ (Sahih al-Bukhari)

## His Humor and Lightness

The Prophet ﷺ was not a stern, humorless ascetic. He laughed, joked, played, and brought warmth and lightness to those around him. His humor was clean, never at the expense of others' dignity, and never involving lies — even in jest.

**The old woman and Paradise:**
An old woman came to the Prophet ﷺ and asked: _"O Messenger of Allah, pray to Allah that He admits me to Paradise!"_
He said with a smile: _"O mother of so-and-so, old women do not enter Paradise."_
She began to cry — and he quickly explained: _"Because they will be made young again before entering."_ (Tirmidhi)

**Racing with Aisha:**
The Prophet ﷺ raced with Aisha (RA) on two separate occasions. The first time she won. The second time he won. He smiled and said: _"This is for that one."_

**The old man:**
A man asked: _"O Messenger of Allah, will you give me a camel to ride?"_
He said: _"I will give you the offspring of a camel."_
The man said: _"What would I do with the offspring of a camel?"_
He said: _"Are camels born from anything but camels?"_ (Abu Dawud)

## His Mercy and Gentleness

The Prophet ﷺ was described as a mercy to all the worlds — and this showed in the most concrete ways.

**With animals:** He forbade the branding of animals on the face, striking them on the face, and causing them unnecessary pain. He narrated that a woman entered Hell because she kept a cat locked up until it died of hunger — and that a man entered Paradise because he gave a thirsty dog water from his shoe.

**With enemies:** At the Conquest of Makkah — when he entered the city with 10,000 soldiers after years of persecution, torture, and warfare — he declared a general amnesty. To his greatest enemies he said: _"Go, for you are free."_ (_Irhabuu fa antum al-tulaqaa'_). No revenge. No score-settling. No summary executions.

**With those who wronged him:** A woman from the tribe of Banu Khuza'ah once tried to poison the Prophet ﷺ at a meal — she confessed when confronted. He forgave her. When he finally died from the effects of that poisoning years later, he mentioned it — but spoke of no punishment for her.

**With the vulnerable:** He visited the sick, walked in funeral processions of non-Muslims, and stood in respect when a Jewish funeral procession passed by. When asked why he stood, he said: _"Is it not a soul?"_

## His Simplicity

Despite leading a state, commanding armies, and being the spiritual authority of a growing civilization — the Prophet ﷺ lived with extraordinary simplicity.

- His bed was a leather mat stuffed with palm fiber. When Umar (RA) entered one day and saw the mat's pattern on the Prophet's ﷺ side, he wept.
- He owned so little at death that his shield was pledged to a Jewish merchant for food.
- He mended his own sandals, milked his own goat, and swept his own home.
- He ate on the floor, refused special chairs or raised platforms, and forbade people from standing for him.
- He said: _"Eat together and mention the name of Allah, and your food will be blessed."_

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sahih al-Bukhari**

Aisha (RA) said: _"The family of Muhammad ﷺ never had their fill of wheat bread for three consecutive days until he died."_

_Not from poverty alone — but from choice. He gave away what came to him._
{% endhint %}

## His Speech

The Prophet ﷺ spoke with remarkable precision and impact. His speech was:

- **Measured** — he spoke at a pace that allowed listeners to count his words. He never rushed.
- **Repeated** — important points were said three times so they would be remembered.
- **Simple** — he spoke to people at their level of understanding. Aisha (RA) said he never spoke in the rapid speech that people today use.
- **Kind** — he never addressed someone by their flaws or weaknesses. He called people by their best names and titles.
- **Honest** — he never lied, even in diplomacy. He said: _"Truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise."_

---

## His Worship

Despite his outward gentleness and lightness, his private worship was intense and continuous.

- He prayed the night prayer (_Tahajjud_) until his feet became swollen.
- When Aisha asked why he prayed so much when Allah had forgiven all his sins, he replied: _"Should I not be a grateful servant?"_
- He fasted more than was obligatory — Monday and Thursday consistently, and three days in the middle of each month (_Ayam al-Beed_).
- He made dhikr in every state — standing, sitting, lying down.
- He wept in prayer. Aisha (RA) reported that his chest sounded like a boiling pot when he wept before Allah.

## Why Muslims Love Him

There is no historical figure in human experience who has inspired the sustained, living devotion that Muhammad ﷺ has inspired in his followers across 14 centuries and 1.9 billion people.

The reasons become clear when you study his life:

1. **He suffered what they suffer.** Loss, grief, persecution, poverty, exile — he bore all of it with patience and grace. His followers in every difficulty find in him a companion who has walked their path.

2. **He loved them personally.** He prayed for his Ummah — the community of all Muslims who would come after him — before he slept. He wept for them before he died. He said: _"My Ummah, my Ummah!"_ in his intercession.

3. **He was entirely human.** He made mistakes and was corrected by Allah. He felt fear and sorrow and longing. He was not a distant perfect being — he was a man who walked with God.

4. **His character was simply beautiful.** Everyone who knew him — including his enemies — acknowledged his trustworthiness, his kindness, and his fairness.

The Prophet ﷺ said: _"None of you truly believes until I am more beloved to him than his own child, his parents, and all of humanity."_ (Sahih al-Bukhari)

This is not a demand for blind devotion. It is the natural consequence of truly knowing him.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The Prophet ﷺ was described by Allah as being "upon a great moral character" — and by Aisha as being "the Quran in human form."
- As a husband, he was devoted, gentle, playful, and practically helpful in the home.
- As a father and grandfather, he showed open physical affection and deep love — radical for his culture.
- He had a genuine sense of humor — clean, kind, and never at others' expense.
- His mercy extended to enemies, animals, and the vulnerable — consistently and without exception.
- Despite political power, he lived in extraordinary simplicity — his shield was pledged for food.
- Muslims' love for him is not blind sentiment — it is the natural outcome of knowing his life.
