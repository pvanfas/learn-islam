# The Madinan Period Building a Civilization

## In This Chapter

> The ten years between the Hijrah (622 CE) and the Prophet's death (632 CE) in Madinah were arguably the most productive decade in human religious history. A persecuted community became a state. A spiritual message became a civilization. This chapter covers the key battles, the Constitution of Madinah, the major treaties, and the transformation of the Muslim community.

---

## Madinah The New Home

When the Prophet arrived in Madinah, he was greeted by the **Ansar** (the Helpers) the Muslims of Madinah who had invited him with joy and celebration. The city would become his home for the rest of his life, and the site of his burial.

His first act was to build the **Masjid an-Nabawi** the Prophet's Mosque with his own hands alongside the Companions. It was simple, made of palm trunks and mud bricks. Today it stands as one of the most magnificent buildings on earth, but its origins were entirely humble.

He then established the **brotherhood** (_Mu'akhat_) between the Muhajirun (the migrants from Makkah) and the Ansar pairing each migrant with a helper who would share their home, food, and livelihood. The Ansar's generosity in this brotherhood is one of the most moving episodes in Islamic history.

---

## The Constitution of Madinah A Pluralistic Governance Document

The **Sahifat al-Madinah** (Constitution of Madinah) was drawn up by the Prophet within months of arriving. It was a written agreement between:

- The Muslim Muhajirun (migrants from Makkah)
- The Muslim Ansar (helpers of Madinah)
- The Jewish tribes of Madinah (Banu Qaynuqa, Banu Nadir, Banu Qurayza)
- And other tribal groups

**Key provisions:**

| Provision                                                    | Detail                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| All signatories form one _Ummah_ one community               | A pluralistic political community across religious lines |
| Muslims and Jews are equal in rights and obligations         | No religious discrimination in civic life                |
| Each group follows its own religious law in internal matters | Religious legal autonomy                                 |
| All parties defend Madinah collectively if attacked          | Mutual defense                                           |
| The Prophet is the final arbiter in disputes                 | Political authority rested with him                      |
| No treaty with Quraysh without mutual agreement              | Collective foreign policy                                |

This document written 1,400 years ago embodied principles of pluralism, legal autonomy, and collective security that political theorists still study today.

## The Major Battles And Their Lessons

The Muslims were attacked repeatedly. Three battles stand out:

### Battle of Badr 2 AH / 624 CE

The first major battle of Islam. A Muslim force of **313** men confronted a Makkan army of **1,000** to prevent an attack on Madinah.

The result: complete Muslim victory. Seventy Quraysh leaders were killed, seventy taken prisoner.

The significance: It established the Muslim community as a serious military and political force. The Quran devoted an entire Surah (_Al-Anfal_ The Spoils of War) to its lessons.

The prisoners were released most after paying ransom, and those who could not pay ransom were released after teaching ten Muslims to read and write. This was a civilizational act: knowledge as the currency of freedom.

### Battle of Uhud 3 AH / 625 CE

A Quraysh force of **3,000** attacked Madinah. The Muslims initially outmaneuvered them but a group of archers disobeyed the Prophet's explicit order not to leave their positions, pursuing the retreating enemy for spoils. The Quraysh cavalry outflanked the Muslim position through the gap left.

The Muslims suffered significant losses including the Prophet's uncle Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib (RA), one of the most beloved Companions, who was killed and his body mutilated.

The Prophet himself was wounded losing two teeth and bleeding from his face. When a Companion called out in anguish: _"The Prophet has been killed!"_ Abu Bakr and Umar responded: _"If Muhammad has died then the Lord of Muhammad has not died!"_

Uhud taught the Muslims the price of disobedience and the nature of trial.

### Battle of Khandaq (The Trench) 5 AH / 627 CE

A coalition of **10,000** soldiers the largest force ever assembled against the Muslims marched on Madinah. The Persian Companion Salman al-Farisi (RA) suggested an innovation: digging a trench (_Khandaq_) around Madinah's exposed northern approach.

The coalition besieged Madinah for weeks but could not cross the trench. Eventually, through diplomatic strategy and a bitter winter, the coalition withdrew in disarray.

The Prophet announced after the battle: _"From now on, we will go to them they will not come to us."_ The tide had turned permanently.

## The Treaty of Hudaybiyyah 6 AH / 628 CE

The Prophet attempted to perform Umrah with 1,400 Companions a pilgrimage, not a military expedition. The Quraysh blocked them at Hudaybiyyah, outside Makkah.

A treaty was negotiated. Its terms appeared humiliating to many Companions:

- Muslims would return without performing Umrah this year
- A 10-year peace was established
- Any Makkan Muslim who came to Madinah without Quraysh permission would be returned to Makkah
- Any Madinan Muslim who went to Makkah would not be returned

Umar (RA) was so upset he went to the Prophet and said: _"Are you not the Prophet of Allah? Are we not on the truth?"_

The Prophet replied: _"Yes."_

Umar: _"Then why are we accepting humiliation in our religion?"_

The Prophet : _"I am the Messenger of Allah and I will not disobey Him, and He is my support."_

Within two years of this treaty, the result became clear: unprecedented access for the Prophet to travel and teach and mass conversions across Arabia. Khalid ibn al-Walid and Amr ibn al-As two of the greatest military commanders in Arab history embraced Islam in the period after Hudaybiyyah.

Allah called this apparently humiliating treaty a _"manifest victory"_:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Fath 48:1**

**Translation:** "Indeed, We have given you a clear opening [victory]..."

_Revealed immediately after the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah what appeared to be a defeat was in reality the gateway to the greatest victory._
{% endhint %}

## Key Takeaways

- The Madinan period (622–632 CE) was the decade in which Islam transformed from a persecuted movement to a complete civilization.
- The Constitution of Madinah established a pluralistic political community one of the earliest written constitutional documents in history.
- The three major battles (Badr, Uhud, Khandaq) each taught essential lessons: reliance on Allah, the price of disobedience, and strategic innovation.
- The Treaty of Hudaybiyyah appearing as defeat was declared by Allah a "manifest victory" and opened the door to the Conquest of Makkah.
- By the end of the Madinan period, the Prophet had transformed a small persecuted community into the dominant political and spiritual force in Arabia.
