# Belief in the Prophets and Messengers

**Difficulty:** Beginner &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~11 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 2 — Aqeedah

---

## In This Chapter

> The fourth pillar of Islamic faith is belief in all of the prophets and messengers sent by Allah throughout history. This chapter explains what prophets are, what distinguishes them, why they were sent, who the major prophets are, and why Muhammad ﷺ is the final one — with a special look at how the Quran portrays the prophets as a connected family of mission.

## Quranic Foundation

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Baqarah 2:136**

**Translation:** "Say: We believe in Allah and what has been revealed to us and what was revealed to Ibrahim, Ismail, Ishaq, Yaqub, and the Descendants — and what was given to Musa and Isa and what was given to the prophets from their Lord. We make no distinction between any of them, and we submit to Him."

_A Muslim must believe in all the prophets without exception. Accepting some and rejecting others is a violation of this pillar of faith._
{% endhint %}

## What Is a Prophet?

A **Nabi** (Prophet) is a human being chosen by Allah to receive divine revelation and convey it to people. A **Rasul** (Messenger) is a prophet who was additionally given a new scripture or a new divine law (_Sharia_) to establish.

All Messengers are Prophets, but not all Prophets are Messengers.

**Why human prophets — not angels?**

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-An'am 6:9**

**Translation:** "And if We had made him an angel, We would have made him appear as a man, and We would have confused for them the matter they are already confused about."
{% endhint %}

Prophets are human so that humanity can relate to them, learn from their example, and see that the divine standard is achievable in human life. An angel being sent would defeat the purpose — humans would say: _"But you're not like us."_

## Qualities of All Prophets

Islamic theology defines four essential qualities of every prophet:

| Quality                   | Arabic  | Meaning                                                        |
| ------------------------- | ------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Truthfulness**          | Sidq    | They never lie — in speech or in conveying revelation          |
| **Trustworthiness**       | Amanah  | They never betray a trust or act dishonestly                   |
| **Conveying the message** | Tabligh | They conveyed everything revealed to them — nothing was hidden |
| **Intelligence**          | Fatanah | They possessed exceptional wisdom and sound judgment           |

And two things are impossible for any prophet:

| Impossible Quality | Meaning                                        |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Lying**          | Kidhb — a prophet cannot lie                   |
| **Treachery**      | Khiyanah — a prophet cannot betray his mission |

**Ismah (Prophetic Protection):** Prophets are protected by Allah from committing major sins that would undermine their credibility as His representatives. This does not mean they never made human mistakes — the Quran honestly records moments where prophets were corrected by Allah. But they were protected from acts of moral corruption.

## How Many Prophets Were There?

The Quran mentions **25 prophets by name**. The Sunnah indicates the total number was far greater — one narration mentions 124,000 prophets in total, though some scholars consider the specific number uncertain. What is certain is that Allah sent guidance to every people:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Fatir 35:24**

**Translation:** "...And there is not a nation except that a warner has passed among them."
{% endhint %}

## The Five Greatest Prophets — Ulul Azm

Five prophets are distinguished in the Quran as _Ulul Azm_ — the prophets of firm resolve — those who faced the greatest trials with the greatest patience:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Ahqaf 46:35**

**Translation:** "So be patient, as the messengers of firm resolve (_Ulul Azm_) were patient..."
{% endhint %}

They are:

1. **Muhammad ﷺ** — the greatest of all creation and the final prophet
2. **Ibrahim (AS)** — tested with fire, exile, and sacrificing his son
3. **Musa (AS)** — confronted Pharaoh with the truth; led his people through the sea
4. **Isa (AS)** — born without a father; tried, accused, and raised by Allah
5. **Nuh (AS)** — called his people for 950 years and only a few believed; survived the flood

## The Prophets as One Family of Mission

One of the most beautiful aspects of the Quranic presentation of the prophets is that they are all given the same essential message — and they acknowledge one another:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Anbiya 21:92**

**Translation:** "Indeed, this is your community — one community — and I am your Lord, so worship Me."
{% endhint %}

Isa (Jesus AS) himself, according to the Quran, foretold the coming of Muhammad ﷺ:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah As-Saf 61:6**

**Translation:** "And when Isa, son of Maryam, said: 'O Children of Israel, indeed I am the messenger of Allah to you, confirming what came before me of the Torah and bringing good tidings of a messenger to come after me, whose name is Ahmad.'"

_Ahmad is one of the names of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ._
{% endhint %}

## Why Muhammad ﷺ Is the Final Prophet

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is _Khatam al-Nabiyyin_ — the Seal of the Prophets:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Ahzab 33:40**

_مَّا كَانَ مُحَمَّدٌ أَبَا أَحَدٍ مِّن رِّجَالِكُمْ وَلَٰكِن رَّسُولَ اللَّهِ وَخَاتَمَ النَّبِيِّينَ_

**Translation:** "Muhammad is not the father of any of your men, but he is the Messenger of Allah and the Seal of the Prophets — and Allah is ever, of all things, Knowing."
{% endhint %}

**Why is no new prophet needed after Muhammad ﷺ?**

1. **The Quran is preserved** — unlike earlier scriptures, the Quran exists in its original Arabic, unchanged. A new prophet is not needed to correct a corrupted scripture.
2. **The Sunnah is preserved** — the Prophet's ﷺ life, words, and actions were meticulously recorded and transmitted through an unbroken chain of scholarship. His example is fully accessible to all generations.
3. **The message is universal** — the Quran was not sent to one people or one era, but to _"all of humanity until the Last Day."_
4. **The Sharia is complete** — the final divine law addresses all of humanity's spiritual, social, and ethical needs.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:3**

**Translation:** "...This day I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favor upon you and have approved for you Islam as religion..."

_This verse was revealed during the Prophet's ﷺ Farewell Pilgrimage — his last Hajj. It was the final declaration that the mission was complete._
{% endhint %}

## How Muslims Refer to the Prophets

Muslims say **ﷺ** (Sallallahu Alayhi wa Sallam — "May Allah's peace and blessings be upon him") after the name of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.

For all other prophets, Muslims say **عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام** (_Alayhis-Salam_ — "Peace be upon him"), abbreviated as **(AS)**.

This is an act of love, honor, and Sunnah — not merely a formality.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Belief in all prophets without distinction is the fourth pillar of Islamic faith.
- All prophets share the same essential message: worship Allah alone and live righteously.
- The Quran names 25 prophets — from Adam to Muhammad ﷺ.
- The five greatest are Muhammad ﷺ, Ibrahim, Musa, Isa, and Nuh — the _Ulul Azm_.
- Muhammad ﷺ is the final prophet — no new prophet will come — because the message is preserved, complete, and universal.
- A Muslim who rejects even one prophet has left the fold of Islam.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<details>

<summary>Why are there no female prophets in Islam?</summary>

The Quran does not explicitly address whether prophethood can be female. The scholars of Ahlus Sunnah hold that all confirmed prophets named in the Quran and Sunnah are male. Some scholars note that Maryam (Mary), Hajar (Hagar), and the mother of Musa received divine communication — but the majority do not classify them as prophets in the technical sense. This is an area of scholarly discussion, not settled unanimity on the _reason_ — the Quran simply records whom Allah chose.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Is it true that the Prophet ﷺ was illiterate? And if so, doesn't that make the Quran more remarkable?</summary>

Yes. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is described in the Quran as _Al-Ummi_ — the unlettered prophet. He could not read or write. This is considered one of the greatest evidences for the divine origin of the Quran — an unlettered man in 7th-century Arabia could not have produced a text of such literary brilliance, scientific accuracy, and theological depth without divine revelation.

</details>
