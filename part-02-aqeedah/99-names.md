# The 99 Names of Allah: Al-Asma al-Husna

**Difficulty:** Beginner &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~14 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 2: Aqeedah

---

## In This Chapter

> Allah has described Himself through His beautiful names, each name a window into who He is, what He does, and how He relates to creation. This chapter introduces the concept of the Divine Names, explains why they matter spiritually, and presents all 99 names with their Arabic, transliteration, meaning, and a reflection on each.

---

## Quranic Foundation

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-A'raf 7:180**

_وَلِلَّهِ الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ فَادْعُوهُ بِهَا_

**Translation:** "And to Allah belong the most beautiful names so invoke Him by them."

_This verse is both a statement and a command: Allah has beautiful names, and we should call upon Him using them. The names are not merely theological. They are the vocabulary of du'a._
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih al-Bukhari and Muslim**

The Prophet said: *"Allah has ninety-nine names, one hundred minus one. Whoever memorizes (*ahsaha*) them will enter Paradise."*

_The word ahsaha means more than mere memorization. It means to enumerate, understand, act by, and call upon Allah through each name._
{% endhint %}

---

## How to Engage with the Names

The spiritual tradition of Islam teaches that engaging with the Names of Allah has three levels:

1. **Memorization:** knowing the name and its meaning
2. **Contemplation:** reflecting on what the name reveals about Allah
3. **Application:** calling upon Allah by the appropriate name in each situation, and reflecting that name in one's own character where possible

The Prophet said: _"Allah is Beautiful and He loves beauty."_ The Names are the most direct revelation of Allah's beauty. They are the Quran's invitation to know Him.

## Three Names to Know Most Deeply

Of all 99, these three are especially emphasized in Quran and Sunnah:

### Ar-Rahman and Ar-Rahim: The Most Gracious, The Most Merciful

These two names open every Surah of the Quran (except Al-Tawbah) and are the names most frequently on the tongue of the believer. Bismillah ir-Rahman ir-Rahim begins every action of significance.

The Prophet said: _"Allah has one hundred mercies. He has sent down one of them to the jinn, mankind, animals, and insects through which they show compassion to one another. He has kept ninety-nine mercies with which He will be merciful to His servants on the Day of Resurrection."_ (Sahih Muslim)

### Al-Hayy Al-Qayyum: The Ever-Living, The Sustainer

These two names appear together in **Ayat al-Kursi**, the greatest verse in the Quran. The Prophet taught that the greatest name of Allah (_Al-Ism al-A'zam_), the name that when called upon, Allah responds, may be these two names. When struck by calamity, the Prophet would say: _"Ya Hayyu, Ya Qayyum, bi rahmatika astagheeth"_ "O Ever-Living, O Sustainer through Your mercy I seek help."

### Al-'Afuw The Pardoner

On _Laylatul Qadr_, the most important night of the year, the Prophet specifically taught the dua: _"Allahumma innaka 'afuwwun tuhibbul-'afwa fa'fu 'anni"_ "O Allah, You are the Pardoner, You love to pardon, so pardon me." He chose _Al-'Afuw_ over _Al-Ghafur_ (The Forgiving) for the greatest night because _'Afuw_ is not just forgiveness, it is complete erasure. As if the sin never happened.

## Key Takeaways

- Allah has 99 beautiful names, each one a window into who He is and how He relates to creation.
- The Prophet said whoever truly enumerates, understands, and calls upon Allah by them will enter Paradise.
- The Names are the vocabulary of dua. Call upon Allah by the name most relevant to what you need.
- Ar-Rahman and Ar-Rahim (mercy) open every act and every Surah. Mercy is the frame in which everything else sits.
- Al-'Afuw (the Pardoner who erases completely) is the name the Prophet specifically prescribed for Laylatul Qadr.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<details>

<summary>Are these exactly 99, or can there be more?</summary>

The Hadith says Allah has 99 names "with which He has named Himself" suggesting these are the revealed names. However, the Prophet also taught a dua asking Allah by _"every name that belongs to You that You called Yourself, or revealed in Your Book, or taught to one of Your creation, or kept to Yourself in the knowledge of the unseen."_ This suggests Allah has names beyond what has been revealed. The 99 are what we know; He may have more that He has kept to Himself.

</details>

<details>

<summary>How do I use the Names in my dua?</summary>

The Quran says: _"Invoke Him by them."_ In practice: when asking for provision, call upon Ar-Razzaq (The Provider). When seeking forgiveness, call upon Al-Ghafur or Al-'Afuw. When afraid, call upon Al-Hafiz (The Preserver) or Al-Wali (The Protecting Friend). When facing injustice, call upon Al-'Adl (The Just). The name you choose should reflect the attribute you are calling upon and this itself is a form of Tawhid: recognizing which of His attributes is most relevant to your need.

</details>
