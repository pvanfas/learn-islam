# The First Generation — Islam in Arabia and Beyond

Difficulty: Intermediate | Part: Part 12 — How Islam Spread

---

The first generation of Muslims — the Companions of the Prophet Muhammad — were the most effective carriers of Islam in history. They did not spread the faith primarily through military campaigns (though military expansion occurred) but through the transformation of their own characters, which made the religion visible and compelling.

The Prophet lived to see the entire Arabian Peninsula accept Islam — a transformation that took 23 years and ended with the Farewell Pilgrimage in 632 CE, when he addressed a gathering of over 100,000 Muslims on the plain of Arafah. Twenty-three years earlier, he had been a single man with a message and a devoted wife. The speed of this transformation was itself a sign.

The Companions carried Islam outward after the Prophet's death. Abu Bakr's caliphate (632-634 CE) reunified Arabia after the apostasy wars. Umar's caliphate (634-644 CE) saw the liberation of the Byzantine-controlled Levant and Egypt and the defeat of the Persian Sassanid Empire — not primarily through military genius, though that was present, but through a combination of military effectiveness and the striking contrast between the Muslims' ethical conduct and the oppressive Byzantine and Sassanid administrations they replaced.

The Coptic Christians of Egypt, long persecuted by Byzantine orthodoxy, largely welcomed the Arab Muslim forces. The Syriac Christians of the Levant similarly found Muslim governance more tolerant than what they had experienced. This social reality — that Islamic governance provided a better deal for minority religious communities than the alternatives — was itself a form of da'wah.
