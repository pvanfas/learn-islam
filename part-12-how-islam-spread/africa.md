# Islam in Africa

Difficulty: Intermediate | Part: Part 12 — How Islam Spread

---

Islam entered Africa through multiple channels over many centuries. The earliest contact was the migration to Abyssinia (Ethiopia) in 615 CE — where the Negus gave sanctuary to the persecuted early Muslims, establishing a relationship of trust between the fledgling Muslim community and Christian Africa.

The trans-Saharan trade routes brought Islam to West Africa by the 9th and 10th centuries. Merchants from North Africa traded gold, salt, and cloth across the desert — and with commerce came the religion of the traders. The great West African empires — Ghana, Mali, Songhai — converted through this commercial contact. Mansa Musa of Mali, whose 1324 Hajj reportedly flooded the Mediterranean gold market, is perhaps the most famous African Muslim ruler in world history.

In East Africa, Arab and Persian traders established coastal settlements as early as the 8th century. Swahili culture — a synthesis of African Bantu culture and Arab Islamic influence — emerged from centuries of commercial and cultural exchange. Cities like Mombasa, Malindi, and Zanzibar became thriving Muslim trading centers.

Sufi orders — particularly the Qadiri and Tijaniyyah — were the primary vehicles of Islamic expansion into sub-Saharan Africa. The Sufi shaykh, with his spiritual authority, healing practices, and education networks, reached populations that no Arab army ever approached. Today, West Africa is home to some of the world's most devout Muslim populations — a direct legacy of peaceful Sufi da'wah.
