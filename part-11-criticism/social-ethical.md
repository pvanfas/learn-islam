## Criticism 1 "Polygamy is Oppressive to Women"

### The Criticism

Islam permits a man to marry up to four wives giving men a right that women do not have. This institutionalizes inequality and enables exploitation.

### Historical Context

**Pre-Islamic reality:** Before Islam, polygamy was unlimited in Arabia and across most of the ancient world. Men took as many wives as they could support or more than they could support. Women had no legal rights in these marriages no Mahr, no right to divorce, no inheritance.

**What Islam did:** Limited polygamy to four then surrounded it with extraordinary conditions.

### The Islamic Ruling The Full Picture

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah An-Nisa 4:3**

**Translation:** "...marry those that please you of women two, three, or four. But if you fear you will not be just, then marry only one..."

**Surah An-Nisa 4:129**

**Translation:** "And you will never be able to be just between wives even if you should strive to do so so do not incline completely toward one..."
{% endhint %}

The Quran permits polygamy in verse 3 and then immediately makes it practically impossible in verse 129 by acknowledging that complete justice between wives is humanly unachievable. The scholarly reading: polygamy is permitted as a last resort in specific circumstances, with full justice as the condition.

**The actual conditions:**

- Financial equality fully providing for each wife equally
- Equal time dividing nights equally (a binding legal obligation)
- Equal treatment in gifts and affection (as much as humanly possible)
- No new marriage if the existing wife negotiated monogamy in her contract

**The historical context of the verse:** Surah An-Nisa 4:3 was revealed after the Battle of Uhud when many Muslim men were killed and widows and orphans needed social and economic protection. The permission to marry up to four was a social welfare mechanism, not a license for self-gratification.

**Reality:** The Prophet was monogamous with Khadijah (RA) for 25 years his most complete, happiest, most documented relationship. His later marriages were almost entirely to widows for political and humanitarian reasons.

## Criticism 2 "Apostasy is Punishable by Death in Islam"

### The Criticism

Classical Islamic jurisprudence includes the death penalty for apostasy (leaving Islam) which violates freedom of religion and conscience.

### The Complexity

This is one of the most genuinely debated topics in contemporary Islamic scholarship and it deserves honest treatment rather than apologetics.

**The classical position:** The four Sunni madhabs historically included the death penalty for apostasy, particularly for public apostasy combined with hostility to the Muslim community (what they called _Riddah_ more than just leaving Islam privately).

**The contemporary scholarly debate:**

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Scholarl disagreement:**

**Traditionalist position:** The classical ruling stands apostasy was treated as treason in the classical Islamic state (where religion and state were unified), not purely as a matter of personal belief.

**Contemporary reformist position (increasingly mainstream):** Scholars like Sheikh Abdullah bin Bayyah, Sheikh Yusuf al-Qaradawi (in later positions), and others argue that:

1. No Quranic verse prescribes the death penalty for apostasy it is derived from specific hadith whose application is debated
2. The classical ruling conflated apostasy with political treason against the Islamic state not pure change of personal belief
3. The Quranic principle of "no compulsion in religion" (2:256) is incompatible with killing someone for a change of belief
4. In a modern context where religion and state are separated, the classical ruling does not automatically apply
   {% endhint %}

**The Quranic perspective:** The Quran mentions apostasy multiple times and in every case, it speaks of punishment in the Hereafter, not in this life. The Quran says _"whoever disbelieves after believing Allah will punish them severely"_ divine punishment, not human execution.

**Historical application:** In Islamic legal history, the apostasy penalty was rarely applied particularly when the individual apostatized quietly without hostility to the community. The strict evidentiary requirements and the focus on community protection made application uncommon.

**The honest position:** This is an area of genuine, ongoing scholarly debate within Islamic tradition. The question of whether the classical ruling applies today in a non-theocratic, multi-religious context is unresolved. The student should follow the ruling of a qualified scholar they trust.

## Criticism 3 "Islam Endorsed Slavery"

### The Criticism

Islam did not abolish slavery and even regulated it, suggesting approval of the institution.

### The Full Context

**Historical baseline:** Slavery was universal in the 7th-century world in Arabia, Persia, Rome, Greece, India, Africa, and everywhere else. Not a single civilization in 610 CE had abolished or considered abolishing it. The question is not whether Islam abolished it in a single decree the question is: what direction did Islamic revelation push the institution?

**What Islam did to slavery:**

1. **Radically improved the treatment of enslaved people:**

- Enslaved people had legal rights they could not be tortured, starved, or killed
- Enslaved people had the right to own property
- Enslaved people could purchase their freedom
- The Prophet said: _"They are your brothers feed them what you eat, clothe them what you wear, and do not burden them with more than they can bear."_ (Sahih al-Bukhari)

2. **Made emancipation an act of worship:**

- Freeing a slave is listed as the expiation for major sins (breaking an oath, accidental killing, etc.)
- Freeing a slave is described as the path to salvation in Surah Al-Balad 90:1213: _"And what can make you know what is the difficult path? It is the freeing of a slave."_
- The Prophet freed his own slaves, encouraged others to do so, and his close Companions freed thousands

3. **The trajectory was toward abolition:**

- Islam forbade new forms of enslavement except war captives effectively stopping the expansion of slavery
- Islam created legal mechanisms for freedom (_Mukataba_ the freedom contract) that enslaved people themselves could initiate
- The Prophet said: _"Whoever frees a Muslim slave, Allah will free every limb of his body from the Fire."_

**The scholarly argument:** Classical scholars like Ibn Ashur and Mahmoud Muhammad Taha have argued that the Quran's overall trajectory the repeated push toward emancipation, the description of freeing slaves as the highest moral act, the universal human dignity (_Karamah_) established in Surah Al-Isra 17:70 shows that abolition was the direction Islam was moving, and that the failure to complete that trajectory was a human failure, not a divine one.

**The honest acknowledgment:** Islamic civilization did not abolish slavery and in some periods and places, Muslim rulers participated in the slave trade in ways that violated Islamic principles. This is a historical failure of Muslim societies not a divine mandate.

## Criticism 4 "The Hudood Punishments Are Barbaric"

### The Criticism

Islamic criminal punishments amputation for theft, lashing for fornication, stoning for adultery are cruel, inhumane, and inappropriate for a modern society.

### The Islamic Framework

**The purpose of Hudood:** The Arabic word _Hadd_ (plural _Hudood_) means "limit" these are the boundary punishments specified by divine text for specific crimes. Their purpose is deterrence making the consequences of certain crimes so severe that the crimes become very rare.

**The extraordinary evidentiary requirements:**

This is what critics almost never discuss. For the amputation penalty for theft to apply:

- The stolen item must exceed a minimum value (_Nisab_) roughly the price of a dinner
- The item must be stolen from a secured location (_Hirz_) not left in a public space
- The thief must not be in genuine need
- Four witnesses must attest to the theft or the thief must confess voluntarily and repeatedly
- The thief must be a free, sane adult

**Historical application:** Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA) suspended the amputation penalty during a year of famine because people stealing out of hunger did not meet the "not in genuine need" criterion. This shows the classical scholars understood these punishments required specific conditions not mechanical application.

**The social conditions argument:** Ibn al-Qayyim al-Jawziyyah (RA) explicitly stated: _"The hand of the thief should not be cut in a time when people are starving."_ Before Hudood can be applied, the Islamic state must have fulfilled its obligations to ensure no one steals out of poverty or necessity.

**The honest position:** Hudood are part of Islamic law but they exist within a framework of extraordinary evidentiary requirements, social justice prerequisites, and judicial wisdom. The application of Hudood by some states without these conditions is a violation of Islamic legal principles, not an application of them.

## Criticism 5 "Islam Discriminates Against Non-Muslims"

### The Criticism

The _Dhimmi_ system the status of non-Muslims under classical Islamic governance imposed the _Jizyah_ tax and various legal distinctions that amount to second-class citizenship.

### The Context

**What Dhimmi status actually provided:**

- Religious freedom the right to practice one's faith, maintain houses of worship, and follow one's own religious law in personal matters
- Property protection non-Muslims' homes, businesses, and places of worship were protected
- Legal autonomy Jewish communities followed Jewish law in their internal disputes; Christian communities followed their canon law
- Military exemption Dhimmis did not serve in the military and paid Jizyah _in lieu of_ military service (Muslims paid Zakat, which funded the military)

**The Prophet's own statement:** _"Whoever harms a Dhimmi (protected non-Muslim) has harmed me."_ (Musnad Ahmad)

**Historical comparison:** The Dhimmi system, in its classical application, provided more legal protection and religious freedom for minorities than virtually any contemporary European system. Jews expelled from Spain in 1492 were welcomed by the Ottoman Empire under Dhimmi protections. Coptic Christians in Egypt maintained their community, churches, and customs for centuries under Muslim governance.

**The honest acknowledgment:** As with all human institutions, the Dhimmi system was applied unevenly across different rulers and periods. Some rulers applied it fairly; others imposed additional restrictions not mandated by Islamic law. The system itself was a medieval compromise not a permanent ideal. Contemporary Islamic scholars broadly agree that the classical Dhimmi framework needs significant evolution for modern multi-religious nation-states.

## Key Takeaways

- **Polygamy:** Permitted with extremely demanding conditions of justice revealed as a social protection measure, not a license for excess. The Prophet was monogamous for 25 years.
- **Apostasy:** A genuinely debated area the classical ruling conflated apostasy with political treason; the Quran's own language speaks of divine punishment, not human execution; contemporary scholars are actively rethinking application.
- **Slavery:** Islam dramatically improved enslaved people's conditions and pushed strongly toward emancipation failure to complete abolition was a human failure, not divine mandate.
- **Hudood:** Exist within extraordinary evidentiary requirements and social justice prerequisites that make mechanical application impermissible. Their purpose is deterrence within a fully just social order.
- **Dhimmi:** Provided significant protections in historical context imperfectly applied, needing evolution for modern contexts, but not equivalent to the caricature of dhimmitude as mere subjugation.
