## In This Chapter

## Criticism 1 "The Problem of Evil How Can a Good God Allow Suffering?"

**The claim:** If Allah is all-powerful, all-knowing, and all-good why does evil and suffering exist? This apparent contradiction undermines the existence of a perfect God.

### The Islamic Response

This is one of the oldest philosophical questions in any theistic tradition and Islamic scholars have engaged with it seriously for over a millennium. Several responses exist:

**1. The Islamic Theodicy Suffering Has Purpose**

The Quran does not present a world in which God makes everything comfortable. It presents a world that is explicitly a test:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Baqarah 2:155157**

**Translation:** "And We will surely test you with something of fear and hunger and a loss of wealth and lives and fruits but give good tidings to the patient. Who, when disaster strikes them, say: 'Indeed we belong to Allah, and indeed to Him we will return.' Those are the ones upon whom are blessings from their Lord and mercy."
{% endhint %}

**2. The Hereafter Rebalances Everything**

The Islamic framework insists that this life is not the complete picture. Every injustice will be answered, every suffering will be recompensed:

_"The world is the prison of the believer and the paradise of the disbeliever."_ (Sahih Muslim)

A world of unlimited pleasure would produce no character, no virtue, no growth. The very existence of hardship is what makes patience, gratitude, and compassion meaningful.

**3. The Limits of Human Perspective**

Allah's knowledge encompasses all of time and all of creation. Human beings see a small slice. What appears to be purposeless suffering from a limited temporal perspective may be part of a larger architecture of meaning that is not visible from within the experience:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Baqarah 2:216**

**Translation:** "...But perhaps you hate a thing and it is good for you, and perhaps you love a thing and it is bad for you. And Allah knows, while you know not."
{% endhint %}

**4. The Source of Evil Is Not God**

Islamic theology holds that evil moral evil originates in the choices of beings endowed with free will. Allah created the capacity for choice; creatures exercise that capacity. A world without moral evil would be a world without genuine moral agents.

**Honest acknowledgment:** This is one of the most difficult philosophical questions in any theistic framework. The Islamic responses above are coherent but they do not eliminate the emotional weight of encountering suffering. Islam does not ask believers to be unmoved by suffering; it asks them to trust the One who sees all of it and to find meaning in their patient endurance.

## Criticism 2 "Muhammad Copied the Quran from the Bible and Other Sources"

**The claim:** The Quran contains stories also found in the Bible and Jewish oral tradition suggesting the Prophet plagiarized from earlier sources he encountered through trade and travel.

### The Islamic Response

**1. The Prophet Was Illiterate**

The Prophet was _Al-Ummi_ unlettered. He could not read or write. The suggestion that he systematically studied the Torah and Gospel, synthesized them, and produced the Quran is contradicted by his own nature.

**2. The Quran Differs Significantly From Biblical Accounts**

Where the Quran and the Bible share stories (Abraham, Moses, Jesus, Mary), they differ in specific, significant details:

- The Quran's account of Jesus's birth under a palm tree (not a manger)
- The Quran's account of Ibrahim not being commanded to sacrifice Ismail in the way described in Genesis
- The Quran's description of Mary being given dates during childbirth a detail not in the Bible but recently confirmed medically as beneficial during labor

If the Prophet was copying from the Bible, why do the accounts differ in ways that cannot be explained by selective reading?

**3. The Quran Explicitly Engages and Corrects Earlier Accounts**

The Quran claims to confirm and correct earlier scriptures not to copy them. It states that earlier texts were altered. This is not the argument of a plagiarist but of a distinct source claiming independent authority.

**4. The Literary Quality Is Not Explainable by Sources**

Even if the Prophet had encountered stories in the Jewish and Christian communities of Makkah and Madinah the literary quality, the theological coherence, the internal structure, and the prophetic claims of the Quran cannot be explained by access to oral traditions about Biblical stories.

**5. The Companions' Testimony**

The Companions people who knew the Prophet intimately, who had every reason to notice if he was drawing on human sources never suggested this. The pattern of revelation (responding to events, answering questions the Prophet had not anticipated, revealing things unknown to all present) is documented consistently across the Seerah.

## Criticism 3 "The Quran Cannot Be the Word of God Because It Endorses Violence"

**The claim:** The Quran contains verses commanding violence against non-believers this is incompatible with a truly merciful God.

### The Islamic Response

**1. Context Is Everything in Interpretation**

The "violent verses" most cited by critics including Surah At-Tawbah 9:5 ("kill the polytheists wherever you find them") were revealed in a specific military context:

This verse was revealed about specific polytheist groups who had broken a treaty with the Muslim community and were actively fighting them. The verse that immediately precedes it says: _"But if they repent and establish prayer and give Zakat, let them on their way indeed, Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."_ (9:5) And the verse after it says: _"If any one of the polytheists seeks your protection, then grant him protection so that he may hear the words of Allah."_ (9:6)

Context which critics almost universally omit transforms the meaning completely.

**2. The Quran Contains More Verses About Mercy Than Violence**

The names _Al-Rahman_ (Most Gracious) and _Al-Rahim_ (Most Merciful) appear 114 times in the Quran at the opening of every Surah except one. Verses about forgiveness, mercy, and compassion vastly outnumber verses about conflict.

**3. Compare With Other Scriptures**

The Bible contains passages commanding the complete destruction of entire peoples (Deuteronomy 7:12, 1 Samuel 15:3) with no contextual limitation. The existence of violent passages in a religious text does not uniquely condemn that religion all ancient scriptures were revealed to specific communities in specific historical contexts.

**4. The Principle of Contextual Reading**

Islamic legal scholars have developed detailed rules for the contextual reading of Quranic verses related to warfare distinguishing between verses that are universal moral principles and verses that were specific to historical military situations. The vast majority of classical Islamic law on warfare prohibits targeting civilians, destroying crops, killing religious figures, and attacking those who have not attacked Muslims.

## Criticism 4 "Islam Is Anti-Science and Opposed to Reason"

This criticism is addressed in detail in the [Islamic Contributions to World Civilization](../part-18-advanced-studies/contributions.md) chapter. In summary:

The civilization that produced algebra (al-Khwarizmi), optics (Ibn al-Haytham), medicine (Ibn Sina), and preserved Greek philosophy through Europe's Dark Ages is not a civilization that was anti-science. The decline of Islamic scientific productivity from the 13th century onward was the result of political fragmentation, the Mongol destruction of Baghdad, and colonial disruption not of Islamic theology.

The Quran itself commands reflection on the natural world as a form of worship making scientific inquiry an act of devotion in the Islamic framework.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The problem of evil has multiple coherent Islamic responses: suffering has purpose (test, growth, recompense), the Hereafter rebalances all injustice, human perspective is limited, and moral evil originates in created free will.
- The Quran-as-plagiarism claim fails: the Prophet was illiterate, the Quran differs significantly from Biblical accounts, and its literary quality cannot be explained by access to oral traditions.
- "Violent verses" in the Quran must be read in context the most cited ones were specific to historical military situations and are surrounded by verses of mercy and specific protections.
- Islamic civilization's Golden Age of science disproves the anti-science charge the Quran itself commands reflection on the natural world.
