# Slavery and Islam Historical Reality and Islamic Response

Difficulty: Intermediate | Part: Part 11 Criticism and Responses

---

## The Criticism

Islam permitted slavery and codified it in Islamic law, perpetuating human bondage for centuries.

---

## Historical Honesty

Islam did not abolish slavery by immediate decree this is historically accurate. When revelation came in 7th-century Arabia, slavery was a universal institution across every civilization on earth. An immediate abolition would have been economically catastrophic and would have left thousands of people born into slavery without any means of support or protection.

---

## What Islam Actually Did

The Quranic trajectory was systematically toward emancipation:

Freeing a slave was prescribed as the highest form of voluntary charity "It is the freeing of a slave" listed first among the acts that constitute taking the steep path toward Allah (Surah Al-Balad 90:13).

Freeing slaves was the expiation (kaffarah) mandated for breaking an oath, for accidental killing, and for the form of divorce called zihar. This created a constant institutional pressure toward emancipation.

Slaves had rights unprecedented in any ancient civilization: the right to purchase their own freedom (mukataba), the right not to be tortured, the right to marry, the right to keep their earnings, protection from being separated from their children.

The children of a slave woman by her master were born free and could not be enslaved. The slave woman who bore her master's child became an umm al-walad she could not be sold and was automatically freed upon his death.

The Prophet said: "Those are your brothers feed them what you eat, clothe them as you clothe yourselves, and do not burden them beyond what they can bear." (Sahih al-Bukhari)

---

## Islam Prohibited New Enslavement of Free People

The transatlantic slave trade's central atrocity capturing and selling free people was explicitly prohibited under Islamic law. Only prisoners of war could become slaves under specific conditions, and even then ransoming and releasing were preferred. The African chattel slavery that European colonial powers developed was categorically prohibited by Islamic legal principles.

---

## The Comparative Standard

The United States maintained slavery for 87 years after independence, with treatment documented as far more brutal than slavery regulated under Islamic law. The Ottoman Empire began restricting the slave trade before the American Civil War ended slavery. This does not excuse failures in the Muslim world but it contextualizes the critique.

---

## Contemporary Position

Slavery is abolished in every Muslim-majority country. No legitimate contemporary Islamic scholar defends its reinstatement. The remaining question whether Islamic law could in principle permit it again under certain conditions is answered by virtually all mainstream scholars in the negative, on the grounds that the objectives of Islamic law (maqasid) require human dignity and that the conditions for the original permission no longer exist.
