# Polygamy The Full Picture

Difficulty: Intermediate | Part: Part 11 Criticism and Responses

---

## The Criticism

Islam permits men to marry up to four wives, which critics argue institutionalizes inequality and the subordination of women.

---

## Contextual History

Polygamy was universal across virtually every ancient civilization in the Hebrew Bible (Solomon had 700 wives, David multiple), in Roman culture, in ancient Persia, in pre-Islamic Arabia without numerical limit. Islam did not introduce polygamy it regulated and restricted it dramatically.

Before Islam: Arab men could marry as many women as they wished with no obligations of equality. After: maximum four, with mandatory equal treatment, equal financial provision, and equal time division.

---

## The Quranic Verse in Context

"If you fear that you will not deal justly with the orphan girls, then marry those that please you of women two, three, or four. But if you fear that you will not be just, then marry only one." (Surah Al-Nisa 4:3)

The verse was revealed after the Battle of Uhud left many widows and orphans. The primary concern: protection of vulnerable women, not male license. The permission is immediately bounded by the condition of justice and the same verse says marrying only one is the recommended default when justice cannot be guaranteed.

---

## The Demanding Conditions

Islamic law requires for a valid polygamous marriage: full equal financial provision for each wife and her children, equal division of nights and time between wives, and just treatment in all things. The Quran itself acknowledges "you will never be able to be equal between wives even if you wish" (4:129) making the bar effectively very high.

---

## The Comparative Question

Western culture prohibits polygamy but permits serial monogamy (multiple partners over time through divorce), cohabitation without legal obligation, and open relationships. In each case, women's formal legal protections are arguably weaker than in a regulated polygamous marriage that carries enforceable financial rights.

The question of which system better protects women is more complex than the dismissive critique suggests.

---

## Contemporary Reality

Most Muslim marriages globally are monogamous. Polygamy is legally restricted in many Muslim-majority countries. The demanding conditions make it rare in practice. The Prophet's own example 25 years of exclusive marriage to Khadijah before her death shows that monogamy was his preferred default.
