## The Companions as the First Transmitters

The Companions of the Prophet were the first generation of Hadith transmitters. They had witnessed the Prophet's words and actions directly and they transmitted what they saw and heard to the next generation with extraordinary care.

Key Companions known as prolific Hadith transmitters:

| Companion               | Narrations |
| ----------------------- | ---------- |
| Abu Hurairah (RA)       | ~5,374     |
| Abdullah ibn Umar (RA)  | ~2,630     |
| Anas ibn Malik (RA)     | ~2,286     |
| Aisha (RA)              | ~2,210     |
| Abdullah ibn Abbas (RA) | ~1,660     |
| Jabir ibn Abdullah (RA) | ~1,540     |

## The Writing of Hadith Was It Permitted?

Initially, the Prophet discouraged writing Hadith to prevent confusion with the Quran during the period of revelation. Later, he permitted it and several Companions maintained written collections during his lifetime.

After the Quran's standardization under Uthman (RA), the prohibition on writing Hadith was fully lifted.

## The Era of Major Collection 2nd3rd Century AH

The systematic collection of Hadith into major works occurred approximately 150250 years after the Prophet though the narrations themselves had been continuously transmitted from the beginning.

Scholars traveled thousands of miles (_Rihla fi talab al-Ilm_ journey in seeking knowledge) to find narrators and verify chains. Imam al-Bukhari reportedly examined 600,000 Hadith before selecting the ~7,000 he included in his Sahih.

## The Science of Narrator Criticism (_Ilm al-Rijal_)

To verify Hadith, scholars developed a rigorous academic discipline biography and assessment of every narrator:

**Questions asked about each narrator:**

- Was their character sound? (_Adalah_ righteousness)
- Was their memory precise? (_Dabt_ accuracy)
- Did they actually meet the person they claim to have narrated from?
- Were their narrations consistent with other reliable narrators?

**Biographical dictionaries** running to dozens of volumes documented thousands of narrators their birth and death dates, teachers, students, and scholarly evaluations.

This system allowed scholars to detect fabrications a narrator claiming to have heard from someone who died before they were born, for example, would be immediately identifiable.

## Fabricated Hadith Why They Entered and How They Were Detected

Fabrication began early during the first civil war (Fitnah), political factions invented Hadith to support their positions. Later, pious storytellers invented Hadith about virtuous acts. Some were invented to attack Islam from within.

**The scholars' response:** The Isnad system made fabrication difficult a fabricator needed to construct an entire believable chain of narrators, all of whose lives and dates could be verified. And the system of _Ilm al-Rijal_ detected inconsistencies.

The Prophet warned severely: _"Whoever attributes to me what I did not say, let him take his seat in the Fire."_ (Sahih al-Bukhari)
