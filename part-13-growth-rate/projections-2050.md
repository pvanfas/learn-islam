## The Pew Research Projections

Based on the 2015 Pew Research Center study "The Future of World Religions":

By 2050: Muslims projected to reach approximately 2.76 billion (29.7% of world population), nearly equalizing with Christians (2.92 billion).

By 2070: Muslims projected to surpass Christians as the world's largest religious group.

Sub-Saharan Africa will be the primary region of growth potentially accounting for more Muslims than the Middle East and North Africa combined by 2050.

---

## The Caveats

Demographic projections carry significant uncertainty over 30-50 year timescales. Factors that could affect outcomes include:

Fertility rate changes as Muslim-majority countries urbanize and develop economically, fertility rates typically decline, as they have in countries like Iran and Turkey.

Apostasy rates if rates of leaving Islam increase, particularly in the Middle East and among younger generations, growth projections would be affected.

Political and social disruptions wars, migrations, and political changes affect demographic patterns unpredictably.

---

## The Islamic Perspective

Muslim scholars are generally careful not to equate demographic growth with spiritual health. The Prophet said: "Islam began as something strange, and it will return to being something strange so glad tidings to the strangers." (Sahih Muslim) suggesting that quality of faith and practice matters more than numbers.

A large Muslim population that does not practice the religion, does not live its values, and does not contribute to human civilization is not the goal of Islamic da'wah. The goal is communities that embody the Quran and Sunnah.
