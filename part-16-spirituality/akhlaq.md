# Virtues and Character (_Akhlaq_) The Moral Foundation of Islam

**Difficulty:** Beginner &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~11 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 16: Islamic Spirituality

---

## In This Chapter

> The Prophet said he was sent to perfect good character. This chapter explores the key virtues of Islamic character Tawadu' (humility), Sabr (patience), Shukr (gratitude), Sidq (truthfulness), Amanah (trustworthiness), and others with their Quranic and prophetic foundations and their practical application in daily life.

---

## Foundation

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Musnad Ahmad (Sahih)**

The Prophet said: _"I was sent only to perfect good character."_
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sunan Abu Dawud (Hasan)**

_"The most complete of the believers in faith is the one with the best character."_
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih al-Bukhari**

_"The heaviest thing placed on the scale of the believer on the Day of Judgement will be good character."_
{% endhint %}

Character (_Akhlaq_) in Islam is not a supplement to worship it IS worship. The Prophet placed good character on the Scale of Judgement it weighs more than many acts of ritual worship.

## The Core Virtues of Islamic Character

---

### 1. Truthfulness (_Sidq_)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih al-Bukhari**

_"Truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise. A man keeps speaking the truth and striving to be truthful until he is recorded with Allah as a Siddiq (great truthful one). And lying leads to corruption, and corruption leads to the Fire. A man keeps lying and striving to lie until he is recorded with Allah as a Kaddhaab (habitual liar)."_
{% endhint %}

Truthfulness is the foundation virtue the one the Prophet was known for before prophethood (_Al-Amin The Truthful_). It encompasses:

- Truthfulness in speech never lying, even in jest (except the three permitted exceptions: reconciling between people, to a spouse for harmony, and in war)
- Truthfulness in dealing no deception in trade or contracts
- Truthfulness in inward state not performing worship for show

**Practice:** When you feel the urge to exaggerate a story, embellish a result, or avoid an awkward truth pause and choose the honest path.

---

### 2. Trustworthiness (_Amanah_)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih al-Bukhari**

_"There is no faith in one who has no Amanah, and there is no religion in one who has no covenant."_
{% endhint %}

Amanah extends far beyond keeping someone else's property safe. It includes:

- Fulfilling all promises and agreements
- Protecting secrets entrusted to you
- Being reliable in all roles employee, employer, spouse, parent, friend
- Not betraying those who have given you access to their trust

The Quran calls trusteeship (_Amanah_) a weight so great that the heavens and earth refused to bear it only humanity accepted it (Surah Al-Ahzab 33:72).

---

### 3. Humility (_Tawadu'_)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih Muslim**

_"Charity does not decrease wealth. Allah increases nothing in a servant who forgives except honor. And no one humbles themselves for the sake of Allah except that Allah raises them."_
{% endhint %}

True humility in Islam is not self-deprecation or false modesty it is accurate self-knowledge: recognizing that all gifts (intelligence, beauty, wealth, knowledge, strength) came from Allah, and that the servant is always returning to dust.

The externals of humility include:

- Not mentioning your own virtues unnecessarily
- Being genuinely pleased when others are praised
- Listening more than you speak
- Treating the poor and the powerful with the same dignity

The Prophet ate on the floor, wore simple clothing, carried his own goods, and would stop to speak with the poorest person who approached him while being the political leader of Arabia.

---

### 4. Generosity (_Karam / Jud_)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih al-Bukhari**

_"The Prophet was the most generous of people, and he was at his most generous in Ramadan."_
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Baqarah 2:261**

**Translation:** "The example of those who spend their wealth in the way of Allah is like a seed which grows seven spikes in each spike is a hundred grains. Allah multiplies for whom He wills..."
{% endhint %}

Islamic generosity is active, not passive it seeks out opportunities to give. The Prophet never turned away a person who asked. He gave away his last food. He distributed all gifts given to him before the day was out.

The categories of generosity in Islam:

- Financial charity (Sadaqah, Zakat)
- Time and skill (helping others, teaching)
- Emotional generosity (patience with difficult people, listening)
- The smile (_"Your smile in the face of your brother is Sadaqah"_ Tirmidhi)

---

### 5. Patience (_Sabr_) Revisited as Virtue

Sabr as a virtue operates in three directions simultaneously:

- **Patience in obeying Allah:** continuing worship even when the nafs resists
- **Patience in avoiding sin:** holding back from what Allah prohibited
- **Patience in hardship:** not complaining or losing trust when trials come

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Az-Zumar 39:10**

**Translation:** "Indeed, the patient will be given their reward without account."
{% endhint %}

The Prophet said: _"No one has been given a gift better and more comprehensive than Sabr."_ (Sahih al-Bukhari)

---

### 6. Gratitude (_Shukr_)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Ibrahim 14:7**

**Translation:** "If you are grateful, I will surely increase you but if you are ungrateful, indeed My punishment is severe."
{% endhint %}

Shukr is the counterpart to Sabr Sabr in difficulty, Shukr in ease. True Shukr has three levels:

- **In the heart:** Recognizing the blessing came from Allah
- **On the tongue:** Saying Alhamdulillah
- **In the limbs:** Using the blessing in ways that please the Giver

The Prophet would pray Tahajjud until his feet swelled and when asked why, given that all his sins were forgiven, said: _"Should I not be a grateful servant?"_ Gratitude is not only for the newly blessed it is the constant orientation of the believer.

---

### 7. Mercy and Compassion (_Rahmah_)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih al-Bukhari and Muslim**

_"The merciful will be shown mercy by the Most Merciful. Show mercy to those on earth the One in heaven will show mercy to you."_
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih Muslim**

_"Whoever is not merciful to others will not be shown mercy by Allah."_
{% endhint %}

The Prophet 's mercy was encompassing to children, animals, enemies, and creation itself. He wept for his Ummah. He showed tenderness to the weak. He forbade cruelty to animals with the same firmness as cruelty to people.

Mercy in practice:

- Overlooking others' mistakes
- Helping without being asked
- Speaking gently when you have the power to speak harshly
- Crying for others' suffering

---

### 8. Justice (_'Adl_)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah An-Nisa 4:135**

**Translation:** "O you who have believed, be persistently standing firm in justice witnesses for Allah even if it be against yourselves or parents and relatives. Whether one is rich or poor, Allah is more worthy of both..."
{% endhint %}

Justice is not merely not committing injustice it is actively standing for it even when costly. The Quran commands justice even against oneself, one's parents, one's family.

The Prophet said: _"A day of justice by a just ruler is better than sixty years of worship."_ Justice is so transformative that it carries the weight of decades of individual piety.

---

### 9. Forgiveness (_'Afw_)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Imran 3:134**

**Translation:** "...and who pardon people and Allah loves the doers of good."
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih Muslim**

_"Allah does not increase a servant in anything when they forgive except honor."_
{% endhint %}

The Prophet forgave those who had tortured his Companions, killed his family, and plotted his death not because he lacked power to retaliate, but because forgiveness was the higher station. At the Conquest of Makkah, when he had total power over his enemies, he declared: _"Go you are free."_

---

### 10. Controlling the Tongue

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sunan at-Tirmidhi (Sahih)**

Mu'adh ibn Jabal (RA) asked the Prophet about the action most likely to lead him to Paradise and away from the Fire. After explaining the pillars of the deen, the Prophet pointed to his tongue and said: _"Control this."_
Mu'adh said: _"O Prophet of Allah will we be held accountable for what we say?"_
He said: _"May your mother be bereaved is there anything that throws people face-first into the Fire except the harvest of their tongues?"_
{% endhint %}

The tongue is the most dangerous limb. Good character with the tongue means:

- Not lying
- Not backbiting (_Ghibah_)
- Not mocking or ridiculing
- Not tale-carrying (_Namimah_)
- Not cursing
- Speaking only what is beneficial

## The Mirror Test Self-Assessment of Character

Ibn al-Qayyim advised this exercise: Look at the 10 people you spend the most time with. The character that appears most frequently among them is likely your own because _"A man is on the deen of his closest friend."_ (Abu Dawud)

Then ask: Are these the characteristics I wish to stand before Allah with?

---

## Key Takeaways

- Good character is worship the heaviest thing on the believer's Scale on Judgement Day.
- The Prophet came to perfect good character his Seerah is the living model.
- The core virtues: Truthfulness, Trustworthiness, Humility, Generosity, Patience, Gratitude, Mercy, Justice, Forgiveness, and Controlling the Tongue.
- Each virtue has its root in Tawhid in the recognition that Allah is the source of all good and the objective is to reflect His attributes in human life.
- Character is built through repetition one truthful choice, one act of generosity, one moment of patience at a time.
