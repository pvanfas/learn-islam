# Al-Ghazali's Ihya Ulum al-Din A Deep Dive

Difficulty: Advanced | Part: Part 16 Islamic Spirituality

---

## The Greatest Work on Islamic Spirituality

The "Ihya Ulum al-Din" (Revival of the Religious Sciences) composed by Imam Abu Hamid al-Ghazali (450-505 AH / 1058-1111 CE) is arguably the most important single work in Islamic intellectual history outside the Quran and the Hadith collections.

Ibn Khaldun, the historian, said: "Were all the books of Islam to be lost except the Ihya, it would be sufficient."

---

## Al-Ghazali's Crisis

Al-Ghazali was the most celebrated scholar in the Islamic world professor at the Nizamiyyah university in Baghdad, lecturer to hundreds of students when, at age 37, he experienced a complete spiritual and intellectual crisis. He stopped eating, could no longer speak to lecture, and entered a state described as near-paralysis. The cause: he recognized that despite his mastery of theology, jurisprudence, and philosophy, his own heart was sick. He was teaching what he did not himself practice. He was pursuing knowledge for status, not for Allah.

He withdrew from his position an act his contemporaries found incomprehensible and spent nearly 11 years traveling, in Sufi lodges, at the Ka'bah, at the Prophet's mosque, in solitary reflection. The Ihya is the result.

---

## The Structure of the Ihya

The Ihya is organized into four volumes, each of ten books:

**Quarter 1 Acts of Worship:** Prayer, Zakat, Fasting, Hajj, recitation of the Quran, dhikr and du'a treated not as rituals but as interior acts with specific spiritual functions.

**Quarter 2 Customs:** Eating, marriage, earning a living, friendship the ethics of daily life, the Islamic approach to the world.

**Quarter 3 Destructive Vices:** The diseases of the heart pride, envy, love of the world, anger, stinginess their signs, causes, and cures. This is the heart of the work.

**Quarter 4 Saving Virtues:** Repentance, patience, gratitude, hope, fear, love, contentment, watchfulness, self-examination, contemplation, and the remembrance of death the positive qualities that lead to Allah.

---

## Key Teachings

Al-Ghazali's central thesis: knowledge without action is worthless, and action without knowledge of the heart is hollow. The revival he sought was not of Islamic law (fiqh) or theology (kalam) in isolation it was of the inner life that gives them meaning.

On intention: Every act of worship can be corrupted by impure intention the same prayer that brings a person close to Allah can drive them away if performed for reputation. The Ihya's treatment of riya (showing off) is the most comprehensive in Islamic literature.

On knowledge: "Knowledge is a light that Allah casts into the heart. That light does not come while there is sin between you and Allah." The prerequisite for real understanding is a purified heart.

On love of Allah: Al-Ghazali's final volume on love (mahabbah) is among the most beautiful passages in Islamic literature describing the love of Allah as the supreme station, the goal of all spiritual work, and the source of every other virtue.

---

## Reading the Ihya Today

The Ihya is available in full English translation the most accessible is the translation published by the Islamic Texts Society (Cambridge), translated by Tim Winter (Abdal Hakim Murad). Reading even one of the 40 books slowly, with reflection and application, is more valuable than reading the entire work as an academic exercise.

Al-Ghazali himself would say: the Ihya is not meant to be read it is meant to be practiced.
