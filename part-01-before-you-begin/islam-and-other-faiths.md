## In This Chapter

> Islam did not appear in a vacuum. It arrived as part of a long chain of divine revelation that Muslims believe has been ongoing since the first human being. This chapter explains how Islam views Judaism, Christianity, and other faiths with both the agreements and the differences stated honestly.

## The Abrahamic Family

Islam, Judaism, and Christianity all trace their spiritual origin to one figure: Ibrahim (Abraham), peace be upon him. Muslims believe he was one of the greatest prophets a man who left his people's idolatry through pure reason, rebuilt the Kaaba in Makkah with his son Ismail, and submitted so completely to God that he is called in the Quran _Khalilullah_ the close friend of Allah.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah An-Nisa 4:125**

**Translation:** "And who is better in religion than one who submits himself to Allah while being a doer of good and follows the way of Ibrahim, inclining toward truth? And Allah took Ibrahim as an intimate friend."
{% endhint %}

All three religions share core commitments: the existence of one God, the importance of moral life, accountability after death, and the guidance of prophets. These are not small overlaps they are foundational.

## How Islam Views the Earlier Scriptures

Muslims believe that God sent **revealed books** to earlier communities. The Quran names four:

| Book                 | Arabic Name | Sent to          |
| -------------------- | ----------- | ---------------- |
| The Torah (_Tawrat_) | التَّوْرَاة | Moses (Musa AS)  |
| The Psalms (_Zabur_) | الزَّبُور   | David (Dawud AS) |
| The Gospel (_Injil_) | الإِنْجِيل  | Jesus (Isa AS)   |
| The Quran            | الْقُرْآن   | Muhammad         |

Muslims believe these scriptures originally contained the same essential message: worship God alone and live righteously. However, Muslims also believe the earlier scriptures were not preserved in their original form they were transmitted through human hands over centuries and underwent alteration, addition, and translation in ways that changed their content.

{% hint style="warning" %}
** Scholarly Note:** This is a theological position, not a dismissal. Muslims hold the **original** Torah, Psalms, and Gospel in great reverence as divine revelation. What exists today the Old Testament, the Psalms, the four Gospels contains truth mixed with human addition and error, according to Islamic teaching. The Quran was sent as the final, protected revelation preserved in its original Arabic to this day.
{% endhint %}

## Islam and Christianity Agreements and Differences

### Where They Agree

| Belief                              | Christianity | Islam |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------ | ----- |
| One God, the Creator                |              |       |
| Jesus (Isa AS) was born of a virgin |              |       |
| Jesus performed miracles            |              |       |
| Jesus is the Messiah                |              |       |
| A Day of Judgement                  |              |       |
| Heaven and Hell exist               |              |       |
| Moral life matters                  |              |       |

### Where They Differ

| Belief                                     | Christianity       | Islam |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------ | ----- |
| Jesus is the son of God                    | Many denominations |       |
| The Trinity Father, Son, Holy Spirit       | Mainstream         |       |
| Jesus was crucified                        |                    |       |
| Jesus died for humanity's sins (Atonement) |                    |       |
| Muhammad is a prophet                      |                    |       |
| The Quran is God's word                    |                    |       |

**On Jesus specifically:** Muslims believe Isa (Jesus AS) was not crucified but was raised by God before crucifixion and that he will return before the Day of Judgement. He is one of the five greatest prophets in Islamic tradition (_Ulul Azm_ the prophets of firm resolve).

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Maryam 19:3033 Jesus speaking as an infant**

**Translation:** "He said: Indeed, I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet. And He has made me blessed wherever I am and has enjoined upon me prayer and charity as long as I remain alive. And [made me] dutiful to my mother, and He has not made me a wretched tyrant. And peace is on me the day I was born and the day I will die and the day I am raised alive."
{% endhint %}

## Islam and Judaism Agreements and Differences

Islam and Judaism share even more structural similarities than Islam and Christianity both reject the Trinity, both emphasize law and practice alongside faith, both have detailed dietary codes (_Halal_ and _Kosher_), and both have a strong tradition of scholarly interpretation of scripture.

### Where They Agree

| Belief                                      | Judaism  | Islam   |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------- | ------- |
| Strict monotheism One God, no partners      |          |         |
| Rejection of the Trinity                    |          |         |
| The Torah was given to Moses                |          |         |
| Prophets as guides for humanity             |          |         |
| Detailed religious law governing daily life |          |         |
| Dietary law and permissible food            | (Kosher) | (Halal) |

### Where They Differ

| Belief                                      | Judaism   | Islam |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------- | ----- |
| Muhammad is a prophet                       |           |       |
| Ismail (Ishmael) was the intended sacrifice | Generally |       |
| The Quran is God's word                     |           |       |
| Jesus is a prophet                          | Generally |       |

## Islam and Other Religions

Islam's relationship with religions beyond the Abrahamic family is more nuanced. The Quran does not explicitly address Hinduism, Buddhism, or other traditions by name but it makes a broad statement:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Fatir 35:24**

**Translation:** "And there is not a nation except that a warner has passed among them."

_Islamic scholars interpret this as meaning God has sent guidance to every community throughout history not only the communities named in the Quran._
{% endhint %}

This has led many Islamic scholars to hold that some truth and divine guidance may exist in other religious traditions, even where Islam disagrees with their theology. The diversity of humanity's religious experience is not viewed as chaos but as evidence of a universal human impulse toward the Divine, which Islam believes finds its clearest and final expression in the Quran.

## How Muslims Are Commanded to Treat Non-Muslims

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Mumtahanah 60:8**

**Translation:** "Allah does not forbid you from being kind and just to those who have not fought you because of religion and have not driven you from your homes. Indeed, Allah loves those who act justly."
{% endhint %}

Islam commands Muslims to:

- Be just and fair with all people, regardless of faith.
- Honor agreements and treaties with non-Muslims.
- Not insult or demean others' religious beliefs.
- Protect the places of worship of non-Muslims living under Muslim governance.

The Prophet had Jewish neighbors, Christian allies, and polytheist relatives. He dealt with all of them with honesty, fairness, and respect even when their political relationships were hostile.

## Key Takeaways

- Islam, Judaism, and Christianity share the same spiritual ancestor: Ibrahim (Abraham AS).
- Muslims revere the prophets of the Bible Moses, David, Solomon, Jesus as genuine prophets of God.
- Muslims believe earlier scriptures contained divine truth but were altered over time the Quran is the final, preserved revelation.
- Islam has the most agreements with Judaism theologically both reject the Trinity and emphasize law and practice.
- The Quran suggests that divine guidance has reached every human community throughout history.
- Muslims are commanded to be just and kind to people of all faiths.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<details>

<summary>Can a Muslim be friends with non-Muslims?</summary>

Yes. The Quran's instruction to avoid taking certain non-Muslims as close allies (_awliya_) refers specifically to those actively at war with the Muslim community a political and military context, not a general prohibition on friendship. Muslims throughout history have had deep friendships, trade partnerships, and intellectual relationships with people of all faiths.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Do Muslims believe non-Muslims go to hell?</summary>

This is a nuanced theological question in Islamic scholarship. The mainstream position is that those who received the message of Islam clearly and rejected it will be accountable. Those who never received the message, or received a distorted version, are a matter between them and Allah who is described 113 times in the Quran as _Al-Rahman Al-Rahim_ (The Most Gracious, The Most Merciful). No Muslim can declare with certainty who is or isn't admitted to Paradise that belongs to Allah alone.

</details>
