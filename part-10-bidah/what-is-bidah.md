# What Is Bid'ah? — Religious Innovation in Islam

**Difficulty:** 🟡 Intermediate &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~11 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 10 — Bid'ah

---

## 📌 In This Chapter

> Bid'ah — religious innovation — is one of the most important and most misunderstood concepts in Islamic scholarship. This chapter defines Bid'ah precisely from the Quran and Sunnah, explains the principle that all acts of worship require evidence, covers the scholarly debate about "good innovation," and gives practical guidance on how to identify Bid'ah in worship.

## 🧭 What Is Bid'ah?

The Arabic word **Bid'ah** (بِدْعَة) comes from the root *Bada'a* — to originate, to create something new without a precedent. The Quran uses this root when it says Allah is *Badi' as-Samawati wal-Ard* — the Originator of the heavens and the earth.

In Islamic law, Bid'ah is defined as:

> **An act of worship introduced into the religion that has no basis in the Quran, the Sunnah, or the established practice of the Companions — and that is presented as an act of worship, a means of drawing closer to Allah.**

The key elements of this definition:

1. **It is in the domain of worship** — not worldly matters
2. **It has no basis** — no text from Quran or Sunnah authorizes it
3. **It is presented as religious** — as something that earns reward or pleases Allah

---

## 📜 The Prophetic Warning Against Bid'ah

The Prophet ﷺ warned against Bid'ah in the clearest and most emphatic terms:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sahih al-Bukhari and Muslim (Khutbat al-Hajah — the Friday Sermon Opening)**

*"The best of speech is the Book of Allah, and the best of guidance is the guidance of Muhammad ﷺ. The worst of affairs are those newly invented. Every newly invented matter (*Muhdathah*) is an innovation. Every innovation is misguidance. And every misguidance leads to the Fire."*

*(Narrated by Jabir RA — Sahih Muslim)*
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith — Sahih al-Bukhari**

*"Whoever introduces into this matter of ours that which is not from it — it will be rejected."*

*(Narrated by Aisha RA)*
{% endhint %}

These two hadith establish the core principle: **anything introduced into the religion that has no basis is rejected by Allah, regardless of the intention behind it.**

## 🏛️ The Fundamental Principle — Worship Requires Evidence

In Islamic jurisprudence, acts of worship and worldly matters follow *opposite* default principles:

| Domain | Default | Implication |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| **Worldly matters** (*Mu'amalat*) | **Permissible** unless prohibited | Cars, phones, new foods — all permitted unless specifically prohibited |
| **Acts of worship** (*Ibadat*) | **Prohibited** unless permitted | Prayer, fasting, specific dhikr — only done in the way the Prophet ﷺ established |

Acts of worship (*Ibadat*) are **Tawqifiyyah** — fixed by divine text. They are not areas for human creativity or innovation. You cannot invent a sixth prayer. You cannot add a Rak'ah to Fajr. You cannot invent a new form of Hajj ritual.

This principle — that worship is determined by the Prophet ﷺ, not by human reasoning — is what makes Bid'ah so serious. The person who practices Bid'ah is, even with good intentions, claiming to improve upon what the Prophet ﷺ left complete.

## 🤔 The "Good Innovation" Debate — Bid'ah Hasanah

One of the most frequently cited arguments for permissibility of certain innovations is the statement attributed to Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA) when he gathered people for Tarawih prayer in congregation:

*"What an excellent innovation (*Bid'ah*) this is!"*

Those who cite this argue that there are "good" innovations (*Bid'ah Hasanah*) and "bad" innovations — and that some religious practices introduced after the Prophet ﷺ can be good.

### The Scholarly Response from Ahlus Sunnah

**The mainstream Ahlus Sunnah position** is that Umar's statement does not establish a general principle of good religious innovation, for the following reasons:

1. **The practice already had prophetic basis.** The Prophet ﷺ himself prayed Tarawih in congregation — he stopped specifically because he feared it would become obligatory. Umar revived a practice with prophetic precedent, not invented a new one. His use of the word *Bid'ah* was linguistic — "something new (to them)" — not the technical religious definition.

2. **Umar's statement is not a source of Sharia.** The Prophet ﷺ said to follow his Sunnah and the Sunnah of the rightly-guided caliphs — and the caliphs themselves were explicit that they followed the Prophet's ﷺ example, not created new religion.

3. **The general Hadith is categorical.** *"Every innovation is misguidance"* is a universal statement. Scholars who accept the concept of Bid'ah Hasanah must find a way to reconcile their position with this categorical statement — which most do by redefining Bid'ah in ways the Prophet ﷺ did not intend.

{% hint style="warning" %}
**🎓 Fair note:** Some serious classical scholars — including Imam al-Shafi'i, Imam al-Nawawi, and Imam al-Suyuti — did use the concept of Bid'ah Hasanah for certain matters. Other major scholars — including Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn al-Qayyim, and Imam ash-Shatibi — rejected this categorization as opening the door to corruption of the religion. Both sides include major scholars. The student of knowledge should understand both positions and follow their madhab's ruling with scholarly guidance.
{% endhint %}

The following are examples that Islamic scholarship discusses as Bid'ah — the details are covered in the dedicated chapter:

| Practice | Discussion |
|----------|-----------|
| **Mawlid** — Celebrating the Prophet's ﷺ birthday | Major scholarly debate — see dedicated chapter |

| **Reciting specific Surahs on specific days** without Sunnah basis | e.g., specific numbers on specific nights beyond what is narrated |

## 🎯 How to Identify Bid'ah

When evaluating a religious practice, scholars ask:

1. **Does it have a basis in the Quran or Sunnah?**
   - *Yes* → It is part of the religion
   - *No* → Proceed to step 2

2. **Is it presented as an act of worship or as something that draws closer to Allah?**
   - *Yes* → It is potentially Bid'ah
   - *No* (it's a worldly matter) → It is not Bid'ah by definition

3. **Did the Companions or the early generations practice it?**
   - *Yes* → It has precedent in the righteous Salaf
   - *No* → It is a strong indicator of Bid'ah

4. **Is there a scholarly consensus against it?**
   - Check with qualified scholarship rather than relying solely on online content

## 🤝 How to Respond to Bid'ah — The Prophetic Method

{% hint style="warning" %}
**🎓 Critical point — avoid extremism in calling out Bid'ah:**

Some people become so focused on identifying Bid'ah that they:
- Alienate family members and community
- Declare entire communities of Muslims as misguided
- Lose sight of their own spiritual development
- Damage community unity over subsidiary issues

The Prophet's ﷺ method was wisdom, patience, and gradual correction — not denunciation and division. A scholar corrects with knowledge and gentleness. A student of knowledge focuses on their own practice before lecturing others.
{% endhint %}

The practical approach:

1. **Learn the Sunnah deeply first.** The best way to identify Bid'ah is to know the Sunnah so well that the Bid'ah becomes obvious by contrast.

2. **Consult qualified scholars** rather than making independent judgments about complex cases.

3. **Correct with wisdom and kindness.** The Prophet ﷺ corrected people with gentleness — harsh denunciation drives people away from knowledge, not toward it.

4. **Focus on the practice, not the person.** Calling a practice Bid'ah is different from declaring the person practicing it as a sinner or as outside Islam.

## ✅ Key Takeaways

- Bid'ah is the introduction of acts of worship that have no basis in the Quran, Sunnah, or practice of the Companions.
- The Prophet ﷺ stated: *"Every innovation is misguidance"* — a categorical warning.
- Acts of worship are Tawqifiyyah — fixed by the Prophet ﷺ, not subject to human creative addition.
- The concept of "Bid'ah Hasanah" (good innovation) is debated — serious scholars hold opposing positions.
- The correct approach to Bid'ah is knowledge of the Sunnah and correction with wisdom — not harshness and division.
- Worldly matters are permissible unless prohibited; acts of worship require explicit authorization.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

<details>

<summary>Is using microphones and speakers in the mosque a Bid'ah?</summary>

No. This is a worldly tool (*wasila*) used to facilitate the prayer — not an act of worship itself. The worship being performed (Adhan, prayer, khutbah) is established by the Sunnah. The tools used to amplify it are worldly means, which are permissible by default. Similarly, using a phone to access the Quran is not Bid'ah — the Quran is the source; the phone is a tool.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Is gathering to read Quran and make dua on certain nights a Bid'ah?</summary>

It depends on what is claimed about those specific nights. Gathering to read Quran and make dua is encouraged generally. If specific nights are designated with specific acts not established in the Sunnah — and presented as a religious obligation or special night — that specific designation becomes problematic. Consult a qualified scholar for specific cases.

</details>
