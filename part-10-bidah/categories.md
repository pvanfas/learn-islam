# Categories of Bid'ah

**Difficulty:** Intermediate &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~7 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 10: Bid'ah

---

## The Main Distinction Worldly vs. Worship

Not everything new is Bid'ah. The critical distinction:

| Domain                                     | Default Ruling                | Examples                                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Worldly matters** (_Mu'amalat_, _Adaat_) | Permissible unless prohibited | Cars, phones, new foods, new medical treatments |
| **Acts of worship** (_Ibadat_)             | Prohibited unless permitted   | Prayer, fasting, specific dhikr, Hajj rituals   |

A new smartphone app is not Bid'ah. Using a new format for an invented prayer on a day the Prophet never designated is Bid'ah.

## The Bid'ah Hasanah Debate

Classical scholars are divided:

**Those who reject Bid'ah Hasanah entirely** (Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn al-Qayyim, majority of Hanbali scholars):

- The Prophet's statement _"every innovation is misguidance"_ is categorical
- Calling any religious innovation "good" opens a door that cannot be controlled

**Those who permit certain innovations** (Imam al-Shafi'i, Imam al-Nawawi, Imam al-Suyuti):

- Distinguish between Bid'ah that contradicts the Sunnah (_Bid'ah Dhammah_) and Bid'ah that does not (_Bid'ah Mahmudah_)
- Al-Shafi'i famously said: "Newly introduced matters are of two types: that which contradicts the Quran, Sunnah, or consensus this is Bid'ah Dalalah (misguidance). And that which is good and does not contradict these this is non-blameworthy."

**The mainstream Ahlus Sunnah resolution:** The label matters less than the analysis. Ask: does this practice have a basis in the Sunnah? Does it contradict an established Sunnah? Does it create a religious obligation not established by the Prophet ?
