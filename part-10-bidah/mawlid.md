# The Mawlid Celebrating the Prophet's Birthday

**Difficulty:** Intermediate &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~10 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 10 Bid'ah

---

## In This Chapter

> The celebration of the Prophet's birthday (_Al-Mawlid al-Nabawi_) is one of the most widely practiced and most intensely debated topics in contemporary Islam. This chapter presents both sides of the scholarly debate honestly, with the evidence for each position, so that the reader can understand why serious scholars hold different views.

## Setting the Context

The Prophet Muhammad was born in 570 CE in Rabi' al-Awwal. His birth was an event of cosmic significance the arrival of the mercy to all worlds.

The question is not whether the Prophet deserves to be honored and celebrated the Quran commands love of him and abundant Salawat upon him. The question is: **does commemorating his birthday in a specific annual gathering have a basis in the Sunnah, or is it a permissible innovation, or an impermissible one?**

This is a genuine scholarly disagreement not a settled matter.

---

## Position 1 - The Mawlid Is Bid'ah (Not Permitted)

**Held by:** Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn al-Qayyim, Ibn Rajab al-Hanbali, Ibn Baz, Ibn Uthaymeen, and many contemporary scholars particularly in the Gulf tradition.

### Core Arguments:

**1. No precedent in the Sunnah or practice of the Companions**

The Prophet never celebrated his own birthday. Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, and Ali (RA) who loved the Prophet more deeply than anyone did not establish such a celebration. The Tabi'un (second generation) did not do it. The Tabi' al-Tabi'in did not do it.

If this was a good act, those closest to the Prophet would have been the first to do it. Their omission of it is significant.

**2. The general principle acts of worship require evidence**

The Prophet said: _"Whoever introduces into this matter of ours that which is not from it it will be rejected."_ (Sahih al-Bukhari)

The celebration of the Mawlid as a religious event is a Muhdath (newly invented matter) it has no textual basis.

**3. Historical origin the Mawlid began in the 4th century AH**

The first recorded organized Mawlid celebration was under the Fatimid Caliphate in Egypt (4th century AH) a dynasty that many Sunni scholars considered heterodox. This is not evidence that it is permissible it is evidence of when and where the practice originated.

**4. The slippery slope concern**

Accepting the principle that "good innovations" are permissible opens the door to potentially limitless innovations each proponent believing their innovation is "good."

---

## Position 2 - The Mawlid Is Permissible (or Recommended)

**Held by:** Imam al-Suyuti, Imam Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, Imam al-Nawawi, Imam al-Shafi'i (by implication), Imam al-Ghazali (indirectly), and many contemporary scholars particularly in the traditional Sunni mainstream.

### Core Arguments:

**1. Quranic basis for honoring the Prophet's birth**

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Yunus 10:58**

**Translation:** "Say: In the bounty of Allah and in His mercy in that let them rejoice. It is better than what they accumulate."

_Some scholars use this verse to justify expressing joy at the greatest divine bounty the Prophet himself._
{% endhint %}

**2. The Monday fast hadith**

{% hint style="info" %}
**Hadith Sahih Muslim**

The Prophet was asked about fasting on Monday. He said: _"That is the day on which I was born, and the day on which I received the first revelation."_

_Scholars who permit the Mawlid argue: the Prophet himself expressed joy at his birthday through fasting showing that marking the occasion of his birth is itself from the Sunnah. The specific form (fasting) may vary, but the principle of marking the occasion is established._
{% endhint %}

**3. Imam al-Suyuti's ruling**

Imam Jalal al-Din al-Suyuti (849–911 AH) one of the greatest scholars in Islamic history wrote a specific treatise defending the Mawlid: _Husn al-Maqsid fi Amal al-Mawlid_. His argument: the Mawlid consists of gathering people to recite Quran, hear the Seerah, praise the Prophet , feed people, and make dua. All of these individual components have basis in the Sunnah. Their combination is not a forbidden innovation.

**4. The principle of Bid'ah Hasanah**

Some scholars including al-Nawawi, al-Ghazali, and al-Suyuti accept that not all newly introduced practices are equally forbidden. They distinguish between _Bid'ah Sayyi'ah_ (bad innovation in matters of belief and worship that contradict the Sunnah) and practices that support the deen without contradicting the Sunnah.

---

## The Middle Position A Reconciliation

Many contemporary scholars articulate a middle position:

**The content matters more than the label:**

- Mawlid gatherings that consist of Quran recitation, Seerah lessons, Salawat on the Prophet , and charitable feeding of the poor represent activities that are all individually Sunnah. If gathered together around the Prophet's birth month as an expression of love scholars who permit Bid'ah Hasanah consider this acceptable.

- Mawlid celebrations that include music, mixed-gender gatherings without Islamic etiquette, excessive praise verging on exaggeration of the Prophet's status, or practices from non-Islamic traditions are impermissible by consensus.

- The **specific belief** that celebrating the Mawlid is obligatory, or that missing it is a sin, or that the Prophet spiritually attends Mawlid gatherings is not supported by evidence and is rejected.

---

## How to Approach This Disagreement

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Important principle:**

Both positions are held by major Ahlus Sunnah scholars of the highest caliber. A Muslim following the position of Ibn Taymiyyah and not celebrating the Mawlid is not an extremist. A Muslim following the position of al-Suyuti and al-Nawawi and attending a Mawlid gathering of Quran and Seerah is not an innovator.

The real danger is:

- **On the prohibiting side:** Condemning those who celebrate as polytheists or grave worshippers this is extreme and unjust
- **On the permitting side:** Allowing Mawlid gatherings to include genuinely impermissible elements under the cover of "celebrating the Prophet "

Muslims should follow the ruling of their qualified local scholar, maintain courtesy toward those who differ, and focus above all on their love of the Prophet being expressed in the Sunnah ways abundant Salawat, following his example, and studying his Seerah.
{% endhint %}

---

## Key Takeaways

- The Mawlid is a genuine scholarly disagreement within Ahlus Sunnah not a settled matter.
- **Position against:** No precedent in the Sunnah or Companions, the principle that worship requires evidence, historical origin in questionable sources.
- **Position for:** The Monday fasting hadith, Imam al-Suyuti's detailed argument, the principle that gatherings of individually Sunnah components are permissible.
- **Consensus:** Mawlid gatherings with Quran, Seerah, and Salawat are more defensible than those with impermissible elements; declaring it obligatory or the Prophet physically attends is rejected.
- **The essential practice:** Whether or not one celebrates the Mawlid, every Muslim should send abundant Salawat on the Prophet , study his Seerah deeply, and love him more than anything in this world.
