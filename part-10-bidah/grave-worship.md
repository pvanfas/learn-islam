# Grave Worship and Tawassul

**Difficulty:** Intermediate &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Read time:** ~8 min &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Part:** Part 10 Bid'ah

---

## The Foundation Death Does Not Transfer Intercession Rights

In Islamic theology, intercession (_Shafa'ah_) is entirely in Allah's hands. He permits certain beings primarily the Prophet on the Day of Judgement to intercede. No dead person can be petitioned to act as an intermediary in the way one petitions Allah directly.

---

## Tawassul Seeking Nearness Through an Intermediary

**Tawassul** (تَوَسُّل) means seeking nearness to Allah through an intermediary. It has permitted and prohibited forms:

### Permitted Forms of Tawassul

| Type                                      | Example                                                          | Status                                   |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Through one's own deeds                   | "O Allah, by my love of You and by this prayer I have prayed..." | Permitted                                |
| Through a living person's du'a            | Asking a living righteous person to make du'a for you            | Permitted (this is just asking for du'a) |
| Through the names and attributes of Allah | "O Allah, by Your name Al-Rahman, have mercy on me"              | Permitted                                |

### The Disputed Form Tawassul Through the Status of the Prophet or Deceased Saints

**Position A (permitted many traditional scholars):** Saying "O Allah, by the status of Your Prophet , grant me X" using the Prophet's high rank as a means of directing the du'a to Allah. This is directed to Allah; the Prophet is mentioned as a means.

**Position B (not permitted majority of Hanbali and contemporary scholars):** This is not established in the Sunnah and risks confusion with asking the Prophet himself. Better to use the permitted forms.

---

## What Is Clear Shirk

**Making du'a directly to the deceased** asking a dead person to grant what only Allah can grant is **Shirk** by scholarly consensus.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Surah Al-Fatir 35:22**
**Translation:** "...Indeed, you cannot make those in graves hear..."
{% endhint %}

The deceased cannot hear du'a (the evidential position of Ahlus Sunnah) and even if they could, granting provisions, health, or forgiveness is not in their power. Directing these requests to anyone other than Allah is Shirk.

---

## Visiting Graves The Correct Adab

**Permitted and encouraged:**

- Visiting graves to remember death
- Making du'a FOR the deceased (_not to them_)
- Saying: _"As-salamu alaykum ahl al-diyar..."_

**Not permitted:**

- Building structures over graves
- Lighting candles at graves
- Prostrating toward or touching graves for blessing
- Making du'a TO the deceased
